# Voice & copy — beanies.family pins

Pin copy is greg's voice, tuned for a visual search engine. Two jobs at once:
**win the search** (keywords Pinterest can index) and **sound like a real dad who built
this**, not a marketing bot. The pin image sells the click; the title + description earn the
ranking.

## The voice (non-negotiables)

- **All lowercase** for titles and descriptions. This is a settled brand rule — the only
  UPPERCASE is the eyebrow/kicker *on the image itself* (e.g. `FAMILY ORGANIZATION`).
- **greg's first-person maker voice.** Plainspoken, warm, a little self-deprecating,
  specific. He's a dad of three who built this for his own family. "get your whole family on
  the same page." "maybe the world's greatest family organization app… that was built
  entirely by accident." Not "Discover the ultimate family organization solution!"
- **No em-dashes.** Use plain hyphens or restructure. Em-dashes read as an AI tell in his
  authored copy.
- **No hype filler.** Avoid "ultimate", "revolutionary", "game-changing", "supercharge",
  "effortlessly", exclamation-mark stacks. If a line could be on any SaaS landing page, it's
  wrong.
- **Privacy is a genuine differentiator** — private, local-first, no accounts, no ads, no
  data harvesting, the family owns an encrypted file. Lead with it where it fits the pin's
  angle; don't bolt it onto every pin.
- **Anything written in greg's voice goes to greg for a pass before it's final** (his
  standing rule for voiced content). Draft it, show it, let him make it his.

## Title (the headline that ranks)

- **Hard limit 100 characters; only ~30–40 show before truncation.** Put the hook + the
  primary keyword in the **first ~40 characters**. Practical sweet spot: 40–70 chars.
- **Write it as a real search phrase**, because that's how it ranks. Lead with what a parent
  would actually type: "weekly family meal plan", "family budget reset", "chore charts for
  kids", "family command center". The Notion strategy lists the target phrases.
- Lowercase, greg's voice, benefit- or outcome-shaped. A number or a concrete promise pulls
  clicks ("the 20-minute sunday money reset").
- **The on-image headline and the pin title can differ.** The image headline is short and
  punchy for thumbnail legibility (the `<em>` keyword is the orange word); the title field is
  the fuller search phrase. Keep them consistent in spirit.

## Description (where the keywords live)

- **Max 500 characters; only the first ~50–60 show before "more".** **Front-load 2–3 natural
  keyword phrases in the first sentence** — descriptions ARE indexed and are a real ranking
  surface, not decoration.
- Then 1–2 sentences of greg's-voice context: what it is, who it's for, the private/local-first
  angle when relevant, and a soft nudge to the blog/guide (not a hard "click here").
- Weave keywords into real sentences; don't keyword-stuff. Read it aloud — if it sounds like
  a human wrote it, it's right.
- **Hashtags: minimize.** They're ~deprecated for ranking in 2026. Default to **none**, or at
  most 2–3 highly-relevant ones at the very end, and only if greg wants them. Always ask
  rather than silently appending the old 3–5 bank. (See the playbook's "hashtags" section for
  the tension with the Notion doc.)

## On-image text (eyebrow + headline + kicker)

- **Eyebrow**: UPPERCASE, Heritage Orange, letter-spaced, 2–5 words — a category or promise.
  Examples that shipped: `FAMILY ORGANIZATION`, `FAMILY PLANS · FINANCES · SCRAPBOOKS`. Use a
  middot `·` to separate a triad.
- **Headline**: lowercase, Outfit 800, short enough to stay legible at thumbnail size (roughly
  3–7 words per line, 2–4 lines). Wrap the single most important word/phrase in `<em>…</em>`
  so it paints Heritage Orange (e.g. the `same page`, `greatest`).
- **Kicker** (optional, photo pins): a small italic lead-in above the wordmark, e.g.
  `why i built`. Lowercase, greg's voice.

## Worked examples (match this register)

**Example 1 — evergreen mascot pin (shipped pin-02)**
- eyebrow: `FAMILY PLANS · FINANCES · SCRAPBOOKS`
- headline: `maybe the world's <em>greatest</em> family organization app`
- subtitle: `…that was built entirely by accident`
- title: `maybe the world's greatest family organization app (built by accident)`
- description: `one private place for your family's plans, money, and memories. a local-first
  family organizer a dad built for his own house of three - no accounts, no ads, your data
  stays in a file you own. here's the story of how it happened.`

**Example 2 — budgeting angle (from the strategy copy bank)**
- eyebrow: `FAMILY BUDGET`
- headline: `the weekly money reset for <em>busy families</em>`
- subtitle: `a 20-minute sunday ritual`
- title: `the weekly money reset for busy families (20-minute sunday ritual)`
- description: `a simple weekly family budgeting routine to reset your spending in 20
  minutes - no spreadsheets, no stress. how one family stays on top of money without a
  finance degree, in a private app you actually own.`

**Example 3 — organization angle**
- eyebrow: `FAMILY COMMAND CENTER`
- headline: `one place for the <em>whole family's</em> plans`
- title: `how we set up a digital family command center (not a wall of paper)`
- description: `family calendar, to-dos, and money in one private view the whole family
  shares. how we run a household of five without a giant wall calendar - and keep it all in a
  file we own, not on someone's server.`

## Anti-patterns (rewrite on sight)

- Title Case or ALL-CAPS titles/descriptions. (Lowercase only; caps stay on the image eyebrow.)
- Em-dashes; exclamation stacks; "ultimate/revolutionary/effortless".
- Generic stock-photo energy or a bare app screenshot as the pin.
- A description with no keyword in the first sentence.
- The same headline recoloured three times and called "3 fresh pins" — vary the angle, not
  just the palette.
