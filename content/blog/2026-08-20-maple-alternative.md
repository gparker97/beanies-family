**📢 BREAKING NEWS 📢**

The [Maple family app](/blog/best-cozi-maple-alternatives-in-2026), one of the biggest VC-backed names in the family organization technology arena (more affectionately known as "FamTech," a term I literally learned today, and instantly fell in love with) has announced that it's **shutting down**. It's over, Johnny. It's over.

As of December 31, 2026, Maple is gone, and as per their shutdown announcement, every bit of user data goes with it.

I was genuinely shocked by this news, because Maple had a serious pedigree: Strong founders (ex-Shopify execs), a slick product, and _actual_ money ($5M in seed funding). I considered them one of the main players in the _FamTech_ (I love that we have that term now) space.

Based on internet reports (which can always be believed), the Maple team was acquired by Wander, a company that's _not_ in the family space at all. Maple was acquired for their team, and Wander apparently has no interest in keeping the app running, so it's lights-out. Unfortunately, that's how these stories end sometimes. I wish the best to their team.

## For Maple users: First things first - get your data out

If you're a Maple user, here's what matters right now:

- Your account (and Maple itself) is up until December 31, 2026
- After that, it's over. User data is **permanently** deleted, with no way to get it back.

Export your data ASAP, including your calendars, lists, notes, and whatever else you can, before the end of the year. Don't leave it until December, however tempting that may be.

## Not for nothing, but hey - this is _exactly_ the thing I've been going on about

Not to say I told you so (I would never do that), but [I wrote a whole guide](/guides/local-first-family-finance-planning-tools) about local-first apps. One of the main reasons to choose local-first is this exact scenario - your favorite software company disappears, and poof! Your data goes with it.

When your data lives on someone else's servers, your family's entire life, activities, schedules, notes, medications, and whatever other little details you spent your time painstakingly cultivating are a business decision away from being jettisoned to oblivion.

Now, here we are. Real families, real data, and a real deletion date on the horizon.

## So getting to the point: I actually liked Maple - what do I do now?

From what I could tell (although I never truly used it in anger) Maple seemed like a genuinely nice family hub - a shared calendar, chores, grocery lists, meal plans, AI, and all that. Some of what beanies does was inspired by Maple. It's the stuff I also think is important to share the mental load, and keep a household from falling apart.

Now, here comes the pitch, so get ready. You knew this was coming.

beanies.family does _all_ that, but goes even further into areas Maple never touched - money, linking your family life with your financial life, accounts, budgets, goals, etc. Also, travel plans (bolstered by AI), lists, family data, allergies, medications, recipes. Even (coming soon) meal plans that can be shared with your family, and lots more. It's a secure, private place, where your whole family can gather and your data is yours.

The real difference is where your data lives. Unlike Maple (and most family organization apps) beanies.family is local-first and fully encrypted, meaning your family's precious personal data lives on **_your storage_, _not mine_**. I can't read it, sell it, or lose it, because I never had it in the first place.

## But.. wait? What about…?

And yeah, I know what you're all probably thinking: "greg, you're one cool dude, but what happens if YOU go away?" Ok, first of all, thanks for thinking I'm cool. And also, how _dare_ you? Like that would ever happen.

But, second of all, yeah, that's kind of the whole point. Let's say I got hit by a bus tomorrow (by the way, why is the go-to IT metaphor for losing somebody always getting hit by a bus? A bit macabre, but sure, let's go with it). Anyway, let's say that I do, in fact, get clobbered by a speeding bus, my body careening across the highway, my bones crushed to a fine powder, bringing the life of an unassuming IT engineer to a violent end. Your data is _still_ not going anywhere. It was always yours.

That's what local-first means, and it's what would have saved every Maple family from the scramble they're in now.
