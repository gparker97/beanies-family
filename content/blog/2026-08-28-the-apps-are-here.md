[I’ve talked about PWAs](https://beanies.family/blog/made-a-wish-to-become-a-real-boy/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=post&utm_campaign=the-apps-are-here&utm_content=pwa-callback) in the past (and if you have zero recollection of that, for which I honestly wouldn’t blame you at all, that’s ok). PWAs are great, and I stand behind that. But the powers that be still lock you out of many key features of native apps, which is why, as I said before, native apps still win the day.

Outside of simply being listed and searchable within the walled gardens of the Apple and Google app stores (along with about 10 gajillion other apps of massively varying quality), there are features of a native app that are genuinely useful for users that would otherwise not be available to us.

For example: _**sharing targets.**_ Anybody who’s ever shared a cat meme to a friend on WhatsApp should know what I’m talking about here. It’s the simple act of sharing something through your phone’s _share_ menu so it can be sent to another app. When it comes to [beanies.family](https://beanies.family/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=post&utm_campaign=the-apps-are-here&utm_content=sharing-targets), it’s one of the easiest ways to share data to the app. Share a picture of an invite, and it shows up directly in your beanies calendar, or share a link to a recipe and bam! It gets added right to your family cookbook, with all the details. Things like this massively simplify our family life, and hopefully, yours too.

Exciting news - I’m already working on building this for our app (it’s a doozy, but it should be ready soon)!

I’m honestly confused about why people don’t talk about this more. Not about the beanies apps, I mean. We’ve already established that nobody cares about that. I’m talking about the _actual process_ of building (and getting the sacred blessing) for a native app. In our case, we (here I go with “we” again) have gone through pages and pages of App Store paperwork, multiple App Review rejections, three consecutive iOS release runs that died because of a stuck version record, plugins that compiled fine but were invisible to the app, and certificate and entitlements issues galore. Who knew that building an iPhone app would be so much work?
