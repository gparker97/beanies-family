What makes a great family choreboard? In my opinion, it’s any choreboard that _actually_ motivates your kids to do their chores!

That’s pretty much all there is to say, though if I stop here my _claude-bot_ will probably scold me for not meeting the requisite weekly blog word count. Luckily, there’s _a lot_ to show.

![The choreboard on the beanie wall: a lane of chores for each of three kids, with sports, lessons, homework and chores like "Clean your room without complaining", and beanies parachuting in over Milo's lane with the banner "Afternoon, all done! Nice work, Milo."](/blog/choreboard-wall-showcase.webp 'wide')

_beanies probably has the greatest humanoid bean-themed choreboard ever made, if i do say so myself_

In all seriousness, raising your kids to be better, more responsible human beings is one of the core challenges of parenthood. [A strong, reliable chores routine](https://beanies.family/guides/family-organization?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=post&utm_campaign=choreboard&utm_content=pillar-family-organization) is a great first step towards that goal. But how do we actually engage our kids to do their chores?

Lots of apps have tried. There’s special lists, animations, points, rewards, and more, but if it’s not built in the right way, it simply doesn’t engage kids the way it should. Even beanies is not immune - I initially used lists as a substitute for a true choreboard. It technically worked, but it wasn’t as engaging as it could be. Chores are their own thing, and a properly done choreboard requires organization, structure, and a special something that’s hard to put your finger on (a flying bean, maybe?).

Given that beanies.family already has the [beanie wall and digital calendar](https://beanies.family/blog/beanie-wall-for-your-whole-family?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=post&utm_campaign=choreboard&utm_content=beanie-wall-post), it has a perfect place to add a kids’ choreboard. The beanie wall is already situated in a place your family (and kids) frequent, and the family looks at it every day. In my house, the beanie wall sits right outside the kitchen, and the kids pass it every day on their way out the door as they’re complaining about not wanting to leave the house (which is a wholly separate issue that I don’t have a solution for. Yet.).

Anyway, as I mentioned earlier, talk is cheap - what can the _beanie choreboard_ actually do?!

_if little beanies actually parachuted into my living room every time i finished my chores when i was a kid, i would probably have done them more often_

There’s so much more to the new beanie wall choreboard than just a list of chores. The [beanies.family](https://beanies.family/?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=post&utm_campaign=choreboard&utm_content=homepage-wall-choreboard) choreboard allows you to organize chores by time of day (morning, afternoon, evening, or anytime), set the hours for each category, build stars for every chore completed, earn rewards, and more.

![The Choreboard page on a desktop: a card for each of three kids with their star path to the weekly reward and their afternoon chores, above a Chores Done chart for the week and a This Week summary.](/blog/choreboard-dashboard-desktop.webp 'wide')

_the chores dashboard is your high level summary of chore progress for the week_

You can even customize the number of stars for each chore, and with both weekly and special rewards that you create, there’s no limit to what your kids can earn. And yes - before you even ask - you can award bonus stars when they’re good, and take stars away when they’re bad (honestly - how else will those naughty buggers learn?).

![The Star Report: chores done by each child on each day of the week, stars and bonus stars, stars by week for the last five weeks, and each child's progress towards the special rewards.](/blog/choreboard-star-report-chores-done.webp 'wide')

_the star report is where you can create rewards, give (or take away) bonus stars, and track your kids’ progress towards their rewards_

And if your kids are at all interested in earning their rewards, they’ll actually improve their counting and math skills at the same time.

_“See Joey, only 12 more stars until your special ice cream reward - and don’t forget, reading for 30 minutes is worth 3 stars!”_

In our house, the weekly reward is their allowance, and special rewards are things like ice cream and indoor playgrounds (which they beg for every weekend). There’s a full star report and dashboard where you can actually go back to check and see if they’ve earned their keep - all the data is there!
