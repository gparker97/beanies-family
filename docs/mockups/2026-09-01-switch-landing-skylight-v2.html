the part where i'm honest

more expensive doesn't mean better

A purpose-built device isn't automatically the better device. The iPad in your drawer was a flagship once. It very likely has a sharper screen, a faster chip and a better touch response than a $280 calendar appliance, and you already paid for it. Giving it a second life on the wall is not a compromise, it's a promotion.

What you do give up is the setup. Skylight arrives ready: no auto-lock to disable, no Guided Access to configure, no notifications to silence. That's ten minutes of fiddling on your side, once, and the setup helper walks you through all of it. After that the tablet does one job and never interrupts you to announce a feature.

"having a comprehensive set of awesome family organization and finance planning related features while also being fully private are two things that are no longer mutually exclusive." - me, in an actual interview, sounding smarter than usual

Skylight does deserve the credit for the idea. They got families to look up from six phones and at one shared wall, and that genuinely works. Keep the idea. Point it at a screen you already own.

have your cake, and eat it too

private doesn't mean less anymore

the same three cards as the homepage security section, because the answer doesn't change.

🙈 encrypted means encrypted

Most apps encrypt your data on their servers, with their keys, which is a security feature. beanies encrypts it before it leaves your device, with your key, which is a privacy feature. I can't read it, sell it, or lose it, because I never had it.

📂 one file, yours

Everything lives in a single encrypted .beanpod file on your Google Drive or device. Lose your PIN and your Recovery Kit? I can't help you. That's kinda the whole point.

🤷 nothing to steal

Even if someone wanted to poke around our dark corner of the internet, there's nothing there but code and app config. Good luck finding anything to leak.

fair questions

things skylight folks actually ask

do i need to buy anything?

A wall mount, if you want it to look tidy. That's the whole shopping list. Any tablet with a browser works: iPad, Android, Fire, the one with the cracked corner.

will the tablet fall asleep on me?

Wall mode holds the screen awake while it's on, and the setup helper walks you through the two device settings that matter: auto-lock set to never, and Guided Access so a small person can't wander into YouTube.

can the kids mess it up?

Not really. The wall is locked by default: anyone can tick their chores, nobody can delete anything. Editing needs a grown-up's PIN, and it relocks itself afterwards.

can the kids see our money?

No. Finances are structurally excluded from wall mode, whoever happens to be signed in. There's no setting to get that wrong.

what if my wifi goes down?

The wall keeps showing your week. Everything is stored on the device, so it renders offline and catches up when the connection comes back. It never goes blank.

okay but what if YOU shut down?

Your data is always yours. It's an encrypted file sitting on your own storage. Whatever happens to me, it's still there, and it's still yours. Which is kind of the whole point.

what does it cost?

One price, with everything in it. No hardware, no tier that unlocks the features you actually wanted, and no ads. Ever.
