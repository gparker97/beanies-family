Celebration card, revised

Round one proposed confetti under the shipped gradient border. The border is now the problem: orange edges already mean three other things, and the celebration style quietly erases the bean's own colour. This revision moves tier 3 off the edge entirely.

Why the border has to go

Three collisions, one of them invisible until you read the CSS

  • Orange edge already means "today". MonthDayCard.vue:160 gives the current day border-primary-500 border-l-[3px] over an orange wash.
  • And "selected". Same file, next line: ring-primary-500 ring-2 ring-inset.
  • And "needs attention". TodoItemRow.vue:64 washes hints, overdue and due-today in Heritage Orange — the CIG's alert colour, since red is reserved for destructive actions.
  • The one nobody spotted: it erases the bean. .is-celebration sets border-left-color: transparent !important. The left edge is where the owner's colour lives — the entire point of the identity change — so today a birthday card is the one card that cannot tell you whose it is.

Underneath all four is one fact: in this palette every colour is already spoken for — orange is alert, Sky Silk is calm background, Terracotta is the gradient partner, Deep Slate is the anchor. Recolouring the border just moves the collision. Shape and texture are unclaimed, so that is where tier 3 should live.

If bunting + 35% is the call

What gets built

  • A confetti flag on the verdict. CelebrationVerdict gains it beside celebrating, so a surface asks rather than deciding for itself.
  • Once per activity per session. A module-level Set of activity ids in useActivityIdentity returns confetti: false on later mounts — a virtualised card re-mounting on scroll must not replay it. Cleared on family change, on the same watcher the blank-colour warning already uses.
  • One component. CelebrationConfetti.vue, scaling bean count from its container width, used by the day timeline, the agenda card and the wall's today card. Not four hand-rolled copies — that is what made the wash drift across seven call sites before.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion. Beans still render, they just do not animate in — the same treatment CelebrationShower already applies.
  • Deep Slate lifts to #4a6274 on dark. Otherwise a quarter of the Pod is invisible on the surface that matters most.
  • Delete the gradient border from .is-celebration, including its border-left-color: transparent !important — that line is what erases the bean. The identity edge then needs no special case on a celebrating card.
  • The bunting is one pseudo-element reading a --behind custom property for the surface it sits on, so it works on the wall's tinted day columns as well as a plain page.
