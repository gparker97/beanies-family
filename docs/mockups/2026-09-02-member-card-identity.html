What ships now

A 5px coloured edge on the left of each card. On the wall it is the member's colour for a solo event and Heritage Orange, dashed, for a shared one. It is the only thing carrying identity, and it fails four ways at once.

the shipped card

→ Two of these edges name a person. The third names a relationship. Nothing on the card says which kind of edge you are looking at.

why it doesn't land

  • Unlabelled. A colour means nothing until the mapping is memorised, and nothing on the card teaches it.
  • Duplicable. Six colours, and the member modal assigns a random one — two beans can collide on the first try, so the mapping can be genuinely ambiguous rather than merely unlearned.
  • Two meanings, one device. The wall's left edge means who; the planner's day and week grids use the same 5px edge for the category colour. The same signal answers two different questions in two places.
  • Colour-only. Roughly 1 in 12 men cannot separate several of these hues at a 5px width. The dashed edge is the only non-colour cue, and it only marks shared.

→ The bottom bar isn't a legend. It is a person filter, and the face on each pill does carry the member's colour — but the active state repaints the pill, and nothing pairs a swatch to a bean. The one surface that could teach the mapping never states it.

the idea

A face identifies, colour reinforces

The fix underneath all three directions is the same swap: the bean's face becomes the identifier and colour becomes the reinforcement, instead of colour carrying the whole job alone. A face is self-teaching, survives duplication, and reads without colour vision.

That gives one structural rule worth stating plainly, because it also solves the shared-versus-solo question for free:

the rule

A card shows the beans the surrounding context has not already named.

  • In the wall's bean lanes, the lane header already is the bean, so a solo card shows no face. A shared card shows the other beans, because that is the new information.
  • In today, agenda and month views there is no lane, so every card shows its owners.

→ A face on a card inside a lane therefore always means “someone else is in this too.” Shared events differentiate themselves without a decorative treatment, which leaves the loud treatment free for genuinely special days.

the decision this forced — now made

The planner's day and week grids used to colour by category while the wall coloured by member, so one device answered two questions. That is settled: hue means the bean on every surface, and category moves to the emoji it already owns. See colour means the bean for the evidence — 95 categories cannot fit in a hue channel, and never could.

A · refinement

Where the faces go

Faces inline on the left push the title right by a different amount on every card, so a column of titles no longer starts at one x. That is the single thing a scanning eye needs. The dashed rule below marks where the first card's title starts; watch it drift.

1 · inline left — as drawn

✕ Four cards, four different left edges. The title column is gone.

2 · anchored right — recommended

✓ One left edge, and the right edge becomes a “who else” column you can scan on its own.

3 · fixed gutter

~ Aligned, but it reserves 54px of every card for something most cards don't have — and in a lane, solo cards carry no faces at all, so the gutter is empty far more often than it is used.

4 · own row, under the title

~ Perfect alignment and no width limit, but every shared card grows a line — costly in a dense lane, comfortable in the sheet.

recommendation

Anchor the faces right, and let only the right padding vary.

  • The left edge never moves. The stack is positioned against the card's right edge, so the title always starts at one x no matter how many beans are on it.
  • Only the wrap point changes. A card with three faces reserves more room on the right, so a long title wraps a little earlier. That is a far smaller readability cost than a shifting left edge, and it degrades gracefully.
  • Cap at three plus a count. Four or more shows three faces and +2, so the reserved width has a hard ceiling and a family of eight can't blow the card apart.
  • It earns a second read. Down a lane, the right margin becomes a column of “who else is in this” that can be scanned without reading a single title.
  • Use option 4 where there is room — the details sheet and the today view at full size — since those are read one at a time, not scanned as a column.
A · tier 3

What counts as a celebration

The category is the reliable signal and should always win. Title matching is the safety net for the events nobody categorised, which in practice is most of them. It needs to be a net, not a dragnet.

matches — celebrates

→ An emoji in the title is the strongest signal there is, and the only one that works in every language.

must not match — stays ordinary

✕ Errands about a celebration are not celebrations. A naive substring match confetti-bombs all three.

  • Category first, always. An explicit celebration category wins outright and needs no guessing. Title matching runs only when the category is absent or generic.
  • Match on whole words, not substrings. Otherwise “partygoer”, “Anniversary Road” and half the words containing “bday” light up.
  • Suppress on errand verbs. A title starting buy, book, order, collect, wrap, plan or pay is preparation, not the day itself. This one rule kills most false positives.
  • Emoji is the best signal and the only universal one. 🎂 🎉 🎈 🥳 🎊 in a title is near-certain intent, and it survives Chinese, beanie mode and every future language.
  • Word lists must be translated. The app already ships Chinese; an English-only list means a Chinese family never sees a birthday card. The list belongs in the translation layer beside every other user-facing string, not hardcoded.
  • Give it an off switch. A guess that cannot be corrected is worse than no guess. The activity form should show the celebration state as a toggle, so a wrong guess is one tap to fix and a missed one is one tap to add.

three consequences worth stating

  • activity.color stops being written. There is no colour picker in the activity form — the field is only ever assigned from getActivityCategoryColor(newCategory) and saved back, so it is a denormalised copy of the category colour, not a user choice. Retiring it costs nothing and stops a stale copy drifting from its category.
  • Heritage Orange keeps one job here. A blended wash means these specific beans; Heritage Orange means everybody. Orange does not become a third kind of owner.
  • The dashed edge stays. It is now redundant with hue and faces — and worth keeping anyway, as the one shared-state cue that survives colour blindness and does not require reading a face.

supporting fix

The legend should be the key it claims to be

The wall's bottom bar is a person filter whose pills carry a coloured face — but the pill itself is painted by selection state, so the colour that matters sits in a 32px circle while a slate or white field surrounds it. Nothing pairs a swatch to a bean. Give each entry the same wash the cards use and the pill becomes the key it was assumed to be, while staying the filter it already is.

supporting fix

One colour, one bean

No visual system survives two beans holding the same colour, and today the member modal assigns one at random from six. Colours already taken are shown as taken and cannot be picked, with the owner's face on the swatch so the reason needs no sentence. New beans take the next free colour instead of a random one, so a family never has to think about this at all.

leo's colour — taken colours are out

M G S

→ Hatched and dimmed means taken. The face says by whom.

two things worth deciding here

  • Six colours is the ceiling on unique beans. Fine for most families, tight for a big one. Either extend the palette to eight or nine, or let the seventh bean reuse a colour and lean on the face to disambiguate — which the face treatment now makes safe.
  • Red is in the member palette. The brand reserves red for destructive actions and hard validation errors, so a bean wearing #ef4444 puts an alarm colour on a child's swimming lesson. Worth swapping for a warmer hue — a rose or coral that reads as a person, not a warning.
separate change

Details drawer — one field, one job

In the wall's sheet, when an event has no location the category takes over that hero cell and prints its name as a word — so one slot means two unrelated things, and the category appears as prose the app never uses anywhere else. The planner's own activity modal has no such fallback: it shows location only when there is one, and gives the category its own coloured badge with its emoji. Fixing the wall aligns the two and frees the category to be shown the way the rest of the app already speaks about it.
