Shared event style

Three directions for an event that belongs to more than one bean, shown in the wall's bean-lane view. Every direction keeps the meaning readable without colour alone, and keeps the shared marker legible at three metres. Same four lanes each time: Leo has a solo lesson, and swim club is shared between Leo and Milo.

A

Hand-holding beans

The chip gets a warm gradient outline instead of a colour rail, and a row of little beans holding hands underneath — the brand's own golden rule, used literally. The faces say who without a second line of text, so a parent reads the sharing at a glance rather than opening the event.

B

Shared ticket

A raffle-stub shape: a notch punched out of the left edge and a perforated orange rail, like one ticket torn for two people. Warm, tactile, and instantly different in silhouette — it reads as shared from across the kitchen even out of focus.

C

Confetti banner

The lightest touch: keep today's rail, tint the chip Heritage Orange, and scatter a faint confetti pattern behind the title with a handwritten "together" tag on the corner. Playful, cheap to build, and it leaves the chip's height untouched.
