Celebration surfaces

Six things from your review of the live build. One is a real bug with an exact cause, one confirms a choice that turned out to be load-bearing, and the rest are calls to make.

1 · The bug, shown rather than described

Bunting is invisible on any card that stayed white

The scallops are notches punched in the colour behind the card, not marks painted on it. Against a member wash they read; against a white card they are white on white. Identical CSS in both cards below — the only difference is the background.

Same markup · same CSS · different background

🎂 Member wash — MonthChip, wall chip, timeline

🎂 White card — agenda card, wall drawer
bunting is there, and invisible
  • MonthChip / WallEventChip / DayTimeline bind identity.style, which sets the wash as the background. Bunting works.
  • ActivityListCard binds identity.edgeStyle and keeps bg-white; WallSheet is bg-white outright. Bunting is painted and cannot be seen.
  • Fix: a celebrating card takes the wash on every surface. That is one line per surface, and it makes the tier consistent instead of special-casing two components.

2 · Your question about the background

Deliberate — and it turns out to be structural

Honest answer: the white in the last mockup was inherited from the old style, which forced --celebration-surface: #fff to sit inside its gradient border. Removing the border removed the forced white, so the card kept its own wash. That fell out of the change rather than being decided separately — but it is the right outcome and I would not undo it now, because your reasoning is exactly right: hue means whose, and a white birthday card is the one card that stops saying so. The bug above makes it more than a preference: the bunting only exists because the card is not white.

3 · Your proposal: make the confetti easier to see

Three ways, and I would not take the one that dims the wash

Reducing the wash does make the beans pop, but it spends the identity signal to do it — a celebration would be the one card whose owner-colour is weakest, which is the same mistake the orange border made. These keep the wash and change the beans instead.

4 · Confetti on the dense cards too

Agreed — with the count scaled, not the rule dropped

You are right that "some cards celebrate and some do not" reads as broken. The reason to scale rather than paste twenty beans everywhere: a month cell can hold four celebrations at once, and at that size twenty each is a texture. Count follows area — 4 on a month chip, 8 on a week block, 14 on an agenda card, 20 on the wall.

5 · The drawer — full surface, clean title, and it moves

Confetti everywhere except behind the words

You are right that putting it behind the title was backwards: the title is the one thing that must stay readable, and the drawer is mostly empty space that could carry the celebration instead. The beans now fall across the whole panel and the title sits on clear ground. Falling, not static, because a drawer is a moment you opened — the calendar cards stay still, so the movement means "you just arrived", not "this is always wiggling".

6 · What gets built

If this is the call

  • Celebrating cards take the wash on every surface, which is what makes the bunting visible. One line each in ActivityListCard and WallSheet.
  • Confetti opacity 35% → 45%, plus a 1.25px halo in the card's surface colour. One rule in CelebrationConfetti, no per-hue tuning.
  • Bean count scales with surface: 4 month · 8 week · 14 agenda · 20 wall and drawer. The density prop already exists; it gains two more steps.
  • Confetti on month and week chips, removing the "dense surfaces get none" rule that made the board look half-finished.
  • Drawer: full-surface confetti, clean title band, on the wall sheet and the activity modal.
  • Fall-in animation on drawers only, once per open, settling at the end. Cards keep the existing settle.
