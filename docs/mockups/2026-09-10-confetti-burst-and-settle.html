Three phases, per your description: a popper fires from a corner, gravity brings the pieces down to the floor where they pile, and only then does a quiet scatter fade in across the card. Replay any block.

The three options

Same pieces, same colours, same drawer. What differs is where the burst comes from.

Why today's cannot be fixed by tuning: its pieces land on fixed scatter positions spread from 8% to 92% down the panel, so they stop in mid-air. No easing makes that look natural, because nothing in the world stops halfway down. Settling on a floor is the whole difference.

Is it heavy? No. Each piece is three nested spans instead of one, so a drawer holds 60 small elements rather than 20, and everything animates transform and opacity only, which stay on the compositor. The three nested boxes are what buy the arc: horizontal and vertical need different easing (drag out, gravity down), and a single transform cannot do that, which is exactly why today's can only ever travel in a straight line.

My pick is two corners. One corner reads slightly lopsided in a wide panel, and the wall drawer is very wide. Two is symmetrical and costs nothing.
