confetti: shape, opacity, burst

Four questions, side by side and moving. Hit replay on any block to watch it again.

1. What is the piece?

Shown at 6× and then at real size, which is the size that matters. The current piece is one 10×7px rounded blob; the proposal is four real confetti forms mixed together, still in the Pod's four colours and still in Pod order.

At real size the bean has about 70 pixels to work with and no room for the notch that makes a bean a bean, so it lands as a generic blob. Mixed forms read as confetti almost instantly, because the variety itself is the signal: rectangles, strips, curls and discs together are a thing nothing else looks like.

2. How much can the card take?

Real text over each, so it is a readability judgement rather than a swatch. The layer already sits behind the text; this is about visual noise, not occlusion.

My vote is 0.30 on cards, keeping 0.45 in the drawer, which is mostly empty space and can carry more. Below about 0.22 the pieces stop reading as confetti at all and just look like dust on the screen.

3. Should cards animate too?

Today a card's pieces drop 10px in 460ms and stop. Proposed: the same burst the drawer gets, at a shorter distance, then a slow drift so the card stays alive.

One caveat, and it is the wall. A kitchen tablet never sleeps, so an infinite drift there means confetti moving in the corner of the room all evening, on every birthday card, forever. I would take the drift in the app and hold it off the wall: wall cards get the burst on arrival and then settle. Reduced motion drops the movement everywhere and keeps the pieces.

4. The drawer: fall vs burst

Same distance, same pieces. The only differences are the easing, the stagger and what happens on landing.

Why today's reads as floating: the easing decelerates into the landing, so the last third of every piece's journey is a crawl. Gravity does the opposite, so the proposal accelerates, lands in about half the time, staggers over 260ms instead of 900ms so it arrives as one burst, overshoots slightly, and then drifts.
