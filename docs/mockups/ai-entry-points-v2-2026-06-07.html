Why two chips, not one button?
The two readers go to different places — "Invite" launches the activity reader, "Travel booking" the itinerary reader. Naming them teaches what "magic" means the instant you read the card, and routes the tap correctly in one step (no second "what kind?" prompt). The hook stays playful; the chips do the explaining.
Surface 2 · new-activity drawer · the crowding question

One coordinated "Quick start", not two stacked banners

The new-activity drawer already opens with the 🏖️ "Planning a trip?" card, then the 🔁/📌 schedule toggle, then the title. Bolting a magic banner on top stacks two full-width cards before a single input (left). The fix: fold magic and the existing trip shortcut into one compact "Quick start" row — adding the capability while reducing the clutter that's there today (middle). Or, for the absolute minimum, dock magic inside the title field with no new banner at all (right).

My recommendation
Ship Solution A (the unified Quick-start row) for the activity modal — it's the only treatment that improves the existing crowding while adding magic, and it keeps the trip shortcut you already rely on. Keep Solution B (title-docked ✨) in your pocket as the fallback if user testing says the Quick-start row reads as too much. The FAB card and the travel banner are unaffected by that choice — they ship as shown.

Shared primitives (unchanged across surfaces): one <MagicShimmer> surface (the orange→terracotta sheen) in two weights — bold (FAB card, Quick-start magic chip, travel banner) and inline (title-docked button). One verb system: hook "beanies can do magic" → action "Perform magic" → chips "Invite" / "Travel booking". The consent → file-picker → review flow underneath is untouched; only the entry surfaces change.
