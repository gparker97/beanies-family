Calendar navigation — arrows that stay put

Stepping a calendar is a repeated tap. Today the desktop planner sizes the period label to its own text, so the arrows slide out from under a finger already coming down. Mobile has never had this — its label absorbs the slack and the cluster is pinned. The job is to give desktop what mobile already has, without moving the month name off the top left.

Two conventions, and only one keeps the month name first: title left, navigation pinned right. That is Apple's, and it is also what beanies already does on mobile.

Option A — navigation joins the view controls recommended

The month name stays the first thing you read. ‹ Today › moves to the right-hand control group, immediately before Month/Week/Day. Its position then depends only on controls whose widths never change, so it is pinned in every locale, at every text size, for every label — no reserved width to keep in step. It also groups the three things that change what you are looking at, and makes desktop behave exactly like mobile.

Option B — arrows flank the grid, wall-style your suggestion

Chevrons sit in reserved gutters either side of the day-name row, exactly as WallDaysView already does on the beanie wall (arrowsInView: true). Also perfectly stable — the gutters are container edges, so nothing about the label can reach them — and the arrows sit beside the thing they actually move. Big, forgiving touch targets, and the app would match the wall.

The trade: on a wide desktop the two arrows end up far apart and far from the title, which is a long mouse journey for a repeated action, and putting controls inside a header row is unusual on the web outside of date pickers. It is strongest on touch, which is exactly why the wall uses it.

Option C — Today ‹ › before the title rejected

The most common web convention (Google, Outlook, Notion) and perfectly stable, because the cluster precedes the label. Rejected on your constraint: it puts navigation, not the month, in the top-left corner.

Recommendation

Option A, applied to the planner and the wall's header views. It is the only one that satisfies all four constraints at once: the month name stays first; the arrows are pinned by structure rather than by a reserved width that a long locale would break; desktop starts behaving like mobile already does; and navigation sits with the other controls that change what you are looking at.

Option B is worth keeping for the wall, where it already exists and where a big edge target on a mounted tablet beats a tidy toolbar. The two are not in conflict — the wall already chooses per view with arrowsInView, and this just means the app picks A and the wall keeps choosing.
