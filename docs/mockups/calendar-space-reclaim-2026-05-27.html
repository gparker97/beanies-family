Where we are after yesterday

The calendar is the hero — but mobile chrome eats ~380px before event one

Yesterday's redesign was the right move: the sticky command bar keeps you oriented and the calendar fills the width. The side effect is a tall stack of fixed chrome on phones. From the top: the app header (hamburger · greeting · bell), then the page's 16px top padding, then the 3-row command bar, then a second sticky layer (the week strip + a separate weekday row). The docked sub-header sits ~16px below the command bar, so events flicker through a dead gap that's too short to read or tap.

Option B · the browsing reclaim

The command bar steps aside while you scroll

Borrowed from mobile Safari and Google Calendar: scrolling down into your events slides the command bar up out of the way; the first upward flick brings it straight back. The day strip can stay pinned (so you never lose your bearings) or hide with the bar — your call. Tap a frame to toggle scrolled state. Reclaims the bar's full height — but only while you're actively reading, so it pairs with A rather than replacing it.

Threshold + direction

Strip can stay pinned

The day-of-week strip is your orientation anchor — leave it pinned while the heavier bar hides, or hide both. Toggle in the plan.

Honour reduced-motion

Slide transition respects prefers-reduced-motion — snaps instead of animating. Re-uses the existing visibility-poll pattern, no new scroll listener leak.

Option C · collapse two layers into one

Fold the weekday/member labels into the strip

Today the week view stacks the strip (Sun 18 · Mon 19 …) and then a separate sticky weekday row (S M T W T F S) — the same seven columns labelled twice. Put the weekday letter on each strip pill and the second row disappears. Same trick on day view: the per-person avatars become a slim single strip instead of a tall member header. ~24px back and one less seam.
