What changes

Today the backup, the compaction and the publish all happen with no indicator at all, and the outcome arrives as a toast that dismisses itself after a few seconds.

The size figures already exist — stats.beforeBytes and stats.afterBytes are computed and put straight into that toast. They deserve better than four seconds.

The modal is for what the family must act on

This is the argument for a modal over a toast. The end of a compaction is the one moment beanies knows exactly who is cut off and why — so the panel names each person, says what they must do, and says what happens if they don't.

A list rather than a sentence, so the same slot carries any other must-read outcome later.

Decisions, so they aren't relitigated

  • Four steps, matching the real phases of compact() — gates, safety copy, compaction, publish. A step that can't fail is decoration.
  • Not closable while running. An interrupted compaction is exactly what the safety copy exists for; better not to invite one.
  • "Done" is a button, not a timer.
  • Heritage Orange, never Alert Red. Nothing is broken and nothing is lost.
  • No confetti. Setup earns a celebration; maintenance doesn't, and the fanfare would fire on a screen opened to fix a problem.

Failure states aren't drawn

They already exist and are already right: a refusal (compaction.refused.*) and a publish failure (compaction.publishFailed) keep their current copy.

Both would render in this same modal in place of the result block, with the failed step marked and the same Done button.
