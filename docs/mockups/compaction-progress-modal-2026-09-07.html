What this changes

Today the whole run is silent — backup, compaction and publish all happen with no indicator — and the outcome arrives as a toast that dismisses itself. The size figures are already computed (stats.beforeBytes / stats.afterBytes) and already put into that toast; they just deserve better than four seconds.

Deliberate choices, so they are not re-litigated

• Four steps, not five. They map to the real phases of usePodCompaction.compact() — the pre-flight gates, the safety copy, the compaction itself, the publish. A step that does not correspond to something that can actually fail is decoration.
• The modal is not closable while it runs (:closable="false", same as setup). A compaction that is interrupted half way is exactly the state the safety copy exists for; better not to invite it.
• "Done" is a real button, not a timer. This is the one moment the family learns who is cut off until they update, and a toast that vanishes is how that got missed.
• Heritage Orange for the "someone is behind" note, never Alert Red. Nothing is broken and nothing is lost; those people simply have not updated yet.
• No confetti. Setup earns a celebration; routine maintenance does not, and the fanfare would fire on a screen the user opened to fix a problem.

The modal is the place for things the family must ACT on

This is the argument for a modal over a toast, and it is greg's: the end of a compaction is the one moment beanies knows exactly who is cut off and why. A toast that dismisses itself after four seconds is the worst possible carrier for "go and open Sophia's iPad". So the panel names each person, says what they must do, and says what happens if they do not — and the user dismisses it themselves. The same slot carries any other must-read outcome, which is why it is a list rather than a sentence.

Failure states

Not drawn here, because they already exist and are correct: a refusal (compaction.refused.*) and a publish failure (compaction.publishFailed) keep their current copy. They would render in the same modal in place of the result block, with the failed step marked and the same "Done" button.
