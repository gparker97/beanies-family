Bringing your calendar across

The one-time Google Calendar import, issue #94. A migration, not a sync: one direction, once, reviewed by the user, and then it ends. The screen that matters is step 2, because it is where a parent decides what happens to events that already exist in Google.

The one idea this flow has to land. Every row states its own outcome. An event you created gets taken over, so beanies edits update the original and Google still holds exactly one copy. An event someone else created gets copied, and beanies says plainly that your edits will stay here. A recurring event is one row with its pattern, never thirty-eight rows.
Note on the two dark panels. They are pinned dark deliberately, so both themes are on screen at once. Everything around them follows your own theme.

Step 3 Confirm, then a toast — no result screen

Bring 31 things across?

This happens once. You can edit or delete anything afterwards.

  • 22 become beanies activities and beanies looks after them from now on. Editing one here updates it in Google.
  • 9 are copied across and left alone in Google, because someone else created them. Your edits will stay in beanies.
  • 4 of the 31 repeat, and each comes across as one activity with its pattern.
  • Nothing in Google is deleted, and nothing is duplicated.

Where the counts come from

The review is the whole feature. After confirming, the import writes everything in one go and says so in a toast, so there is no fourth screen to build or maintain.

What is worth arguing about. The chip wording is the whole feature in two words. "beanies takes it over" reads as ownership without saying "adopt", which means nothing to a parent. "Copied across" is honest that a second thing now exists in beanies while the Google event is untouched. The invited explainer appears in the review list and persists on the imported activity, so it is written as a sentence that still makes sense months later rather than a one-off warning.
