Magic beans: pick a category, or don't

The drawer stays as shipped: paste is the hero, camera and file sit beneath. The row at the bottom changes from a statement into an optional pick. Every chip below is live: tap to choose, tap again to hand the decision back to beanies. Each direction is shown at three, four and seven categories, because three is today, four is the transactions reader, and seven is where this is going.

AWrapping chips

Pills that wrap to a second line. Nothing scrolls, nothing hides. Height grows by one row at a time, which the sheet can afford because the row sits at the bottom.

BScrolling rail

One row, always the same height, faded edges hint that there is more. Familiar from every phone keyboard suggestion strip. The seventh category is one flick away rather than in view.

CTappable tiles

Today's tiles, kept, made selectable. They already carry the meaning "what beanies can make", so the change to learn is smallest. The grid re-flows from a single row to two as categories grow.

What all three share

  • Nothing selected is the default, and it looks like the default: quiet slate chips, no empty slot, no placeholder.
  • One pick at a time. Tapping the selected chip clears it. The state line under the row says what will happen.
  • Selected uses Heritage Orange on light and the accent lift on dark. It is the only orange in the sheet besides the tagline.
  • A pick is authoritative: beanies reads the content as that kind. The "not right?" banner still works afterwards.
  • Only categories whose reader is enabled and permitted for this member are rendered. A member without finances never sees transactions.
  • The seven shown are illustrative. Activity, trip, recipe exist; transactions is #107; the rest are placeholders to prove the layout.
Lean: A Wrapping keeps everything visible and needs no gesture. At seven it costs one extra row, which the sheet has room for. Rail B hides options; tiles C get cramped on narrow phones once labels are translated.
