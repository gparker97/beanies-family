recipe form — sectioning study

the add/edit recipe modal is eleven field groups in one flat scroll. course, “good for” and tags landed between the source link and notes, which reads as arbitrary. three ways to break it up, and the grouping i’d ship.

the grouping i’d ship

ordered by what a person does first, not by the shape of the data. on the capture path the top two are already filled in and the job is to check them; on the hand-typed path they are what you came to write.

🍽️ the dishname (required) · subtitle · prep / cook / servings · link 🥄 how to make itingredients · steps 🔖 finding it againcourse · good for · tags ✏️ your own touchnotes · photos

why the break falls where it does. the heading between “servings” and “ingredients” lands exactly where the form changes character — short inputs give way to two big textareas — so the eye gets a rest at the one place it needs one. “finding it again” is the load-bearing name: course, meals and tags are not obvious fields, and a heading that says what they are for does more for discoverability than any amount of layout. the one judgement call is the source link, which sits at the end of “the dish” as provenance — it is auto-filled on capture and glanced at while you check the name, not something you fill in later.

recommended

A · labelled rules

one continuous scroll, each group named with a hairline running off to the right. keeps the form feeling like one recipe card rather than four settings panels, adds almost no vertical cost, and every field stays visible so nothing new can hide. trade-off: the lightest separation of the three — scroll fast on a phone and you can pass a heading without registering it.

B · inset cards

each group becomes its own card on the modal surface. the strongest separation, and the groups survive a fast scroll. trade-off: four lots of padding and border make the modal noticeably taller and start to read as a settings screen rather than a recipe card — the one thing the brief says not to lose. on dark it also needs a third surface tier, so inputs sit two steps down from the panel.

C · collapsible

essentials open, the rest folded away with a summary of what is inside. much the shortest scroll. trade-off, and it is the deciding one: it hides precisely the fields nobody knows exist yet. course, meals and tags would ship folded shut on day one, and a field you have to open a drawer to find is a field that stays empty. it also hides the “filled in for you” hint, which is the one thing a captured recipe most needs you to read.
