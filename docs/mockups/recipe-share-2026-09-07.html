Send a recipe to anyone, and let them keep it

One message carries the recipe as readable text and a link. The link opens the sharer’s own version of the recipe, already parsed, with one action to keep it. Recipe data rides in the URL fragment, so it never reaches a server. Colours, type and shapes are the CIG’s; the warm paper is the cookbook’s own #fbf3e3 / surface-paper.

1Where sharing starts

Share joins the existing action row on the recipe hero, beside Edit and “I cooked this”. It is quiet rather than a third gradient button: on this page the gradient is reserved for the one action that records something.

Why the text as well

Most people who receive this will never tap the link, and a bare URL in a group chat is spam. With the recipe written out, the message is a gift whether or not anyone installs anything — and the link becomes the quiet offer underneath it rather than the whole point.

Choosing the receiving surface

 A · Recipe firstB · Offer card on top
First impression The dish. A slim line says where it came from, then the recipe starts. A marketing card. The recipe begins below the fold on a phone.
If they never sign up They still got exactly what their friend meant to send. They got an advert with a recipe attached.
Conversion The sticky bar is visible the whole way down, so the offer is always one tap away without ever being in the way. Higher up-front prominence, at the cost of reading like a landing page.
Verdict Chosen It is what greg asked for in the issue — “the recipe first and the invitation second, never a sign-in wall in front of the content” — and it is the version a person would actually want to be sent. The sticky bar gives up nothing: the offer is permanently on screen. Rejected. It converts the favour into an ad, which is the fastest way to make people stop sharing.
