Scan to sign in

Tracker #97 — cold-device sign-in surfaces. Scanning from a device that is already signed in becomes the first-class way into a beanpod; the recovery kit stays as the last resort. Every surface is drawn for light and dark.

Vocabulary, per the CIG. Opening a family is two steps: unlock the beanpod (family-wide) and then sign in (per person, PIN). "Recovery" names the printed kit and nothing else — so the scan flow is an unlock, never a "recovery". Headings name the object as the beanpod; body copy explains what that is.

1. Cold entry approved direction

The screen a person meets on a device that has never opened this beanpod. The cold device shows its own code and a signed-in device scans it, because the camera is almost always on the phone and the cold device may well be a laptop. One QR, one instruction, identical on both form factors. The code is shown immediately, so the common case needs no decision at all, and the two real fallbacks are still named in plain words rather than hidden behind one vague link.

Why the kit sits at the very bottom. Six of twenty-two families redeemed a kit, every one on a cold device, and five of those six then replaced a PIN that was working — they did not need recovery, they needed a way in. Demoting the kit is the whole point of the issue. It is still one tap away, and the wording never calls it "recovery" for anything other than the printed kit itself.

The passphrase field is conditional. Offer copy derives from envelopeCapabilities(); a family born without a passphrase never sees this field, and a kit-born family is never offered a password it does not have.

"Making a new code stops the last one working" is not optional copy. The token is deliberately never stored, so the app cannot re-show a live code — a second tap genuinely revokes the first. There is no way to make this idempotent, so the sentence is the fix.

Reject is as easy as approve. The corpus is consistent that a one-sided approval sheet trains people to tap the bright button. Both actions are full-width and equally reachable, and the destructive-looking one is not red — under the CIG, red is for deleting things, not for declining them.

6. The moment the family is created one code, then a gate

This screen already gives the recovery kit a QR and gives the way in a line of select-all text. That is backwards, and it sits exactly where families leak: someone finishing setup on a laptop who is about to install the app. Only the sign-in code is scannable here — the kit keeps its own QR on the saved sheet, where it is actually needed. Two codes side by side read as alternatives, and they are not: one is for the next minute, the other for a bad day next year.

The gate opens on an action or an acknowledgement, never on a verification. Saving, printing, or ticking the box all release the Continue button. beanies cannot know whether a PDF really reached a safe place, and pretending otherwise would strand someone at the last step of onboarding if a platform's save silently failed — the worst place in the product to trap a new family.

Only one QR on screen. The kit still carries its own QR on the saved sheet, which is what makes scanning it possible later. Showing both here would make them look like a choice between two ways in.
