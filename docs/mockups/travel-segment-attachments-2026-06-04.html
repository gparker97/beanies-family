Attach images & PDFs to travel segments

Keep the original booking or itinerary right on the segment — an airline e-ticket PDF, a hotel confirmation screenshot. Built on the same photo pipeline activities already use, extended to also hold PDFs. This mockup shows every surface at desktop and phone widths, and offers three treatments for the new PDF "document tile" and two for the "has attachment" indicator — pick one of each.

The "has attachment" indicator

On the collapsed timeline card, a quiet signal that this segment carries documents — so you can see it at a glance without expanding. Pick A or B. Both sit left of the status pill and disappear entirely when a segment has zero attachments.

The attach control — in the edit drawer & wizard

A new "Booking documents" section inside the segment edit drawer (and the same control in the create wizard). It's the existing dashed photo strip, widened to accept PDFs — image thumbnails and PDF document tiles sit side by side, with add buttons and drag-and-drop. Camera capture stays image-only.

Drop an image or PDF to add · or tap a tile to view

What's accepted

Images (jpg/png/webp/heic) and PDFs up to 10 MB. A clear toast appears for anything too big or the wrong type — never a silent drop.

Where it lives

Stored encrypted in your family's Drive, just like photos. Offline? It queues and uploads when you reconnect.

PDF document tile — three treatments

Image attachments already have an established thumbnail look. PDFs are new and need their own tile. Pick A, B, or C. All show the original filename and a 📄 cue; the difference is personality. (Brand note: PDF accent uses Terracotta/Orange, never Alert Red.)

Recommended
ABoarding-pass stub
A little ticket stub — Heritage Orange→Terracotta top edge, a perforated tear line, and a "PDF · filename" footer. On-brand and playful; reads instantly as a travel document.
📄
PDF United_eticket
BClean doc card
Minimal white square matching the photo thumbnail footprint exactly: a 📄 glyph, a small Terracotta "PDF" badge, and the truncated filename. Calm and utilitarian.

PDF

CFolder tab
A manila-style file with a Terracotta tab — leans into the "filed paperwork" metaphor. Warm and tactile, slightly more decorative than B.

Viewing a PDF
