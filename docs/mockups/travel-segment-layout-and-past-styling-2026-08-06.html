Travel segment layout & past styling

Three fixes for the trip timeline, each shown as before → option so you can compare on the same card. Everything stays inside the existing ocean-teal travel identity — no new palette.

Problem 1 · layout The key info is buried Date and time sit below airline, flight #, from/to and terminal. The thing you scan for first is last.
Problem 2 · bug Ongoing stays look finished A hotel is muted the moment its check-in passes — even mid-stay. "Past" should mean check-out has passed.
Problem 3 · legibility Past plans are hard to read The past treatment fades the whole day to 55% + desaturated — it reads as disabled, but you still need to read past plans while travelling.

1 · Put “when” first, then a natural order

Same flight on every card: NH 802, Singapore → Tokyo Narita. The current order leads with the airline; both options lead with the date and time, then fall into a natural when → where → which → reference order.

💡
My lean: Option A. The when-band is the one bold element (per our "spend boldness in one place" rule), it removes 3–4 rows, and departure/arrival side-by-side is genuinely how you read a flight. Option B is the safe fallback if you'd rather not introduce a new block. The same pattern generalises: a stay shows check-in → check-out, a car shows pick-up → drop-off, an activity shows a single starts cell.

2 · An ongoing stay is “now”, not “past”

Today is Thu, 6 Aug. The hotel checked in on the 3rd and checks out on the 8th — you are staying there right now. Today it's grouped under its check-in day (the 3rd) and muted as past. The fix: classify a spanning segment by its end date, and mark a live one.

A multi-night stay is classified by check-out. While today is inside the stay it gets the Heritage-Orange "now" dot + a live chip — matching the "you are here" language already used for today's day-marker. It only goes past after the 8th.

3 · Past plans: subtle indicator, not a fade

A genuinely past day (the outbound flight, now that we've landed). It should read as done at a glance while staying fully legible — you still open past segments for terminals, refs and addresses mid-trip.

Rail vocabulary across the three states — teal solid dot = upcoming · Heritage-Orange halo dot = today / staying now · grey ✓ dot = done. One consistent rail tells the whole past → now → future story, and the segment text never drops below readable contrast.
