The wall, on a screen it currently refuses

The wall asks for 600 CSS pixels on both axes. The entire budget 8-inch Android tablet class lands at 533 or 601 on its short side, and then loses more to the browser toolbar and the Android status and nav bars. This page shows what the wall would look like if it said yes, at each of those widths, so the step-down can be judged before it is built.

Every dimension that changes between the three tiers below is a custom property, so what you are looking at is the proposed rule, not a hand-tuned drawing of it.

The wall today

1280 × 800

The baseline. A mounted iPad or a 10-inch Android tablet, at the sizes the wall ships with today. Worth noticing before we go smaller: the date already truncates here, because the labelled view switcher and the 11.5rem clock reservation between them take more of the header row than the date has left. That is today's behaviour, not a drawing artefact.

A 601px short side

961 × 601

Fire HD 8, and the Lenovo if it shipped the generous density. One pixel over the current bar before any browser chrome exists, which is why it fails in practice. The date and clock step down, the view switcher drops its labels, three columns.

A 533px short side

853 × 533

The Lenovo on the tighter density, and every 8.7-inch Samsung. The compact tier: the subtitle and nav label give up their space, the clock keeps only the time, and the peripheral cards become one scrolling row of chips.

What she sees instead

853 × 533

The same device today. Note the copy: it tells her to open it on a tablet, which is exactly what she did, and it says "wider" when the axis that failed is the short one.

  • Nothing drops below 0.75rem. The CIG floor is 12px and the compact tier's smallest text is 0.75rem, so Large reading mode still has room above it.
  • Tap targets stay at or above 2.4rem (38px). Below that a mounted tablet stops being usable by a child standing at it.
  • Heritage Orange stays the only accent. Today's column and the now-line are orange to terracotta; nothing here uses red, which stays reserved for destructive confirmations.
  • Every surface has a dark partner. Toggle the theme above: the wall ground, the plot, the cards and the chips each move down the elevation ladder, and the accent gets lighter rather than darker.
