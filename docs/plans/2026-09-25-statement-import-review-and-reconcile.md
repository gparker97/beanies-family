# Plan: Magic beans reads bank statements into Finance Corner, with a review-and-reconcile step

> Date: 2026-09-25
> Related issues: Notion tracker #107 (no GitHub issue: direct implementation)
> Plan file: `docs/plans/2026-09-25-statement-import-review-and-reconcile.md`
> Mockup: `docs/mockups/statement-import-review-2026-09-25.html` (direction B, paired card, approved by greg 2026-09-25: "clearer as you see the two side by side, and the copy is more concise, which is very important as this could potentially be many lines")

## User Story

As a parent who tracks the family's money in beanies, I want to hand over my bank statement or a screenshot of recent transactions and have beanies read it, show me what it found next to what I already entered, and let me approve the result, so that keeping Finance Corner current takes minutes a week and never creates duplicates of the transactions I set up myself.

## Context

Magic beans reads invitations, travel documents and recipes today (`MAGIC_READERS` in `src/composables/useMagicReader.ts:63-70`) but nothing financial. Every bank transaction is typed by hand, and the people who most want to stay on top of money stop doing it. A Discord early adopter (a QuickBooks bookkeeper, verbatim feedback on the Notion row) asked for exactly this: AI-read statements, weekly if possible, reviewed before posting, duplicates flagged for approval. The Budget page has shown a "CSV Upload: Coming Soon" tile for months (`src/pages/BudgetPage.vue:675-697`); this work closes that promise.

Families already create their own monthly and ad-hoc transactions (recurring items materialise real rows via `recurringProcessor.ts`; future instances are shown as in-memory projections by `useProjectedTransactions.ts`), and those rows are linked to activities, goals and loans. An import therefore has to find what already exists and let the user merge or skip it. `Transaction.isReconciled` exists but has no UI and is forced `true` only for balance adjustments (`transactionsStore.ts:463-464`); this feature gives it a meaning.

Three real statements were read during pre-plan (Google Drive `Projects/beanies.family/AI/Test bank statements`; they are PII and are NOT committed):

| Statement                                             | Pages | Transaction pages                                  | Lines | Format facts                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ----- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Standard Chartered SG Visa, 20 Aug to 18 Sep 2026     | 6     | 1 to 5 (6 is terms)                                | ~178  | Unsigned purchases, `CR` suffix on credits (refunds, `GIRO PAYMENT 5,513.74CR`), transaction + posting date, MYR/USD original amount as a sub-line under an SGD column, a bank `Transaction Ref` per line, scrambled PDF text layer (columns land in separate blocks) |
| Chase Premier Plus Checking, Aug 2026                 | 2     | 1 (2 is disclosure)                                | 6     | Signed amounts (minus = out), running balance column, a wire description spanning six lines, three identical `Mspbna ACH Trnsfr -5,000.00` lines on one day, no per-line reference                                                                                    |
| Bank of America Visa Signature, 19 Aug to 18 Sep 2026 | 8     | 3 only (1, 2, 4 to 8 are terms, marketing, coupon) | 18    | Sectioned (Payments and Other Credits negative, Purchases positive, zero-value Interest lines), 4-digit reference per line, US `MM/DD` dates with no year                                                                                                             |

Two cross-statement facts matter: the Chase `Bank of America Payment -99.01` and the BofA `ONLINE/MOBILE RECURRING -739.20` are one transfer relationship seen from two sides, and every statement prints the holder's full account or card number, name and address.

Pre-plan decisions (greg, 2026-09-25):

1. A merged row keeps the user's name; the bank's raw description is stored in a new optional field, searchable, shown as "on your statement as".
2. The family's merchant → category history IS sent to the extraction model as context (supersedes ADR-030's "single document only" for this kind; ADR amended, consent copy says so).
3. Every imported or merged row stores an import fingerprint; a later overlapping import shows those lines as already imported and unticked.
4. Provenance is light: import id, statement period, source type per row. The document is not stored.
5. Mockup direction B (paired card) approved, with bulk selection and bulk decisions confirmed as required ("select/unselect all options and the ability to make decisions in bulk").

## Requirements

1. Accept a bank or card statement as PDF, photo or screenshot, CSV, or pasted text, from the magic beans sheet (paste, camera, file) and from the Budget page's import tile. The in-app picker must accept `.csv` and `.txt` in addition to images and PDF.
2. Extract per line: transaction date (the posting date is captured but not used), the bank's raw description, a friendly merchant name, amount and direction, currency and original foreign amount when shown, the bank's reference when present, a line kind (`purchase | payment | refund | fee | interest | transfer | other`), and a suggested category drawn from the family's own category list.
3. Send, with the statement, a compact list of the family's merchant names and the category each was last filed under (never amounts, dates, accounts or members), so suggestions match categories the family already uses. This applies on both the managed and BYOK tiers.
4. Ask which account the import belongs to, pre-selected from the statement (card last 4 digits against `Account.cardLast4`, else institution + currency match, else the only account in that currency), changeable in the review.
5. Read long statements completely: one read per transaction page (PDF/images) or per text chunk (pasted text and CSV), after an on-device page classifier drops pages that carry no transactions. Tell the user exactly what was read and what was not, and let them read a dropped page anyway. Never silently truncate.
6. Before the read, show a consent sheet specific to statements: what is sent (the pages plus the merchant list), how many reads it costs, that nothing is saved until confirm and that the document is not kept.
7. Run an on-device duplicate sweep against real and projected transactions of the chosen account: similar amounts (rounded or approximate), nearby dates (recurring items charged mid-month), similar descriptions, same direction. Never require an exact amount on an exact date. One existing row can be claimed by at most one statement line.
8. Show the review step (mockup direction B): header with statement facts, account row, running totals (to add, look familiar, skipped), a bulk bar, day-grouped rows, each with tick, friendly name, raw description, category chip, amount in Outfit (Heritage Orange out, soft green in), a Sky Silk chip for an original foreign amount, a transfer chip naming the other account when one is detected. A familiar line is rendered as a paired card: the statement line on the left ("On your statement"), the existing beanies entry on the right ("Already in beanies"), amounts side by side, and a three-way choice (merge, keep both, skip) across the bottom, merge pre-selected. Copy on cards stays terse (this list can run to hundreds of lines). Already-imported lines are dashed, unticked, and excluded from counts. A tapped row expands an inline editor for date, amount, category, account and type.
9. Bulk actions: "Tick all" / "Untick all" for the list and a tick toggle on each day header for its group; "Merge all" / "Keep all" / "Skip all" applied to every familiar card at once, each card still individually overridable afterwards. Already-imported lines are never swept up by a bulk action (they are ticked only individually). Defaults: unmatched lines ticked to add; familiar lines merge; fingerprint matches skip.
10. Merge keeps the existing row's id, name and links (`activityId`, `recurringItemId`, `goalId`, `loanId`), takes amount and date from the statement, stores the raw description, marks the row reconciled and fingerprinted. Merging into a projected (not yet materialised) recurring instance creates that instance with the statement's values and its `recurringItemId`, exactly as "this only" does in `TransactionsPage.vue:679-690`.
11. A single confirm commits everything: adds are one atomic worker batch (rows plus balance increments); merges go through `transactionsStore.updateTransaction` so goal and loan side effects are reversed and re-applied correctly; a post-batch read-back verifies the adds are visible. Partial failure is reported per outcome, never silently.
12. Every added or merged row carries `importFingerprint`, `importId`, `importSource`, `importPeriod` and `isReconciled: true`. A later import computes fingerprints the same way and marks matches as already imported.
13. Gate the transactions reader on `canViewFinances`, not `canEditActivities`. A member without the finance permission never sees the tile, the Budget import tile, or the review.
14. The Transaction view shows "on your statement as: …" when the raw description exists; editing a transaction no longer resets `isReconciled` to `false`.
15. Help Center: a new how-to for importing a statement, and the magic beans article's "what we send" section updated for the merchant list. ADR-030 amended.
16. Telemetry per run (never per row) reporting pages classified and dropped, chunks read and failed, lines read, matches found, already-imported count, the user's final add/merge/skip mix and how many defaults they overrode, and the commit outcome.

## Important Notes & Caveats

- **PII in every statement.** Name, address, full account/card number appear on page 1 of all three samples. The model is asked to return only the last 4 digits; the client never logs descriptions, references, amounts or account fields; telemetry carries counts only (allowlisted keys `action`, `kind`, `count`, `detail`, `error_code`). The sample PDFs must never be committed as fixtures; unit fixtures are synthesised.
- **ADR-030 changes for this kind only.** Requirement 3 sends family data. Amend `docs/adr/030-private-ai-tiered-architecture.md` (the "Data-minimization" bullet at `:57`, "Send only the single document the user pointed at, never the family dataset") with a dated update naming exactly what is added (merchant name + category id, top 150 by frequency, no amounts/dates/accounts/members), and update the two code comments that restate the invariant (`src/services/ai/types.ts:68-69`, `src/services/ai/documentExtractionService.ts:13-14`), the consent copy (`ai.consent.whatValue` stays for other kinds; a statement variant is added), and the help article `how-beanies-ai-handles-your-photos` (`security.ts:884-885`, "Never the rest of your family's data").
- **Consent timing.** `MagicBeansDoor` asks consent BEFORE the picker (`MagicBeansDoor.vue:152-165`), when the page count is unknown, and `ingestInAppSource` REQUIRES a branded `ConsentGrant` (`useSharedDocumentIngest.ts:1538-1542`; the brand exists so an ungated read is a compile error). For hint `transactions` the door defers consent until after the pick and classification so the sheet can say "5 reads". To keep the compile-time guarantee rather than loosening the parameter to `undefined`, the door passes a second branded value: `ingestInAppSource(input, consent: ConsentGrant | DeferredStatementConsent)` where `DeferredStatementConsent` is minted only by `deferConsentForStatement()` in `useDocumentConsent.ts`. **The union goes no deeper than the top of `runIngest`.** `runIngest` narrows it in its first lines: if `statedKind(source) === 'transactions'` it hands off to the statement branch (which never consumes either value, see below) and returns; otherwise a deferred value refuses out loud via `notReady(env, 'consent_deferred_wrong_kind', …)` (unreachable while the door's own `hint === 'transactions'` check holds, pinned by a test) and only a real `ConsentGrant` reaches `read()`. `read()`, `classify()`, dispatch and everything below keep their `ConsentGrant` signature untouched, so the deferred type is a two-line concern rather than a second grant type threaded through the spine. The statement branch ALWAYS mints its own grant with `requestConsent({ kind: 'transactions', reads })` once the count is known, because the merchant list is a new disclosure that no generic grant covers; a generic grant that arrives via the unhinted path is simply not reused. `requestConsent()` currently takes no arguments (`useDocumentConsent.ts:79`); it gains an optional `{ kind, reads }` held in a module-level `consentRequest` ref, and the single mounted modal (`DocumentExtractConsentModal`, no props, mounted once in `App.vue:2124`) switches its "what we send" items on it exactly as it already switches "where it goes" on `tier`. A family with `skipDocumentConsentPrompt` still sees the statement variant the first time (new disclosure), acknowledged via a new family setting `aiStatementConsentAcknowledgedAt` persisted through the existing `persistAiSetting` helper (`settingsStore.ts:512`, which already toasts on failure); after that the skip applies. The two skip rules live in ONE predicate `shouldSkipPrompt(opts)` inside `requestConsent` so there is a single place that decides whether the sheet shows. `onConsentConfirm` writes the acknowledgement when `consentRequest.kind === 'transactions'`, in the same try/finally that already guards the remember-persist so a persist failure never strands the caller.
- **The unhinted and corrected paths are ONE path.** If someone pastes statement text or picks a CSV without choosing the Transactions tile, the share classifier returns `kind: 'transactions'`; if someone taps "not right? → Transactions" in another review modal, the correction arrives with `to: 'transactions'`. Both land in the same statement branch of `runIngest`: the first read (if any) was made under the generic consent without the merchant list, so its lines are discarded; the app shows the statement consent sheet ("This looks like a bank statement") and reads the ORIGINAL source (the `documents` File, the pasted text, or the correction's already-prepared page images) as the `statement` task. The unhinted share prompt for this kind returns only `{ kind, account, period }`, no lines, to keep that call small. A correction to `transactions` skips the share re-read entirely (there is nothing to gain from a lineless answer) and goes straight to the statement branch; the free-correction token stays unspent and `MagicMiscategorisedBanner` shows its non-free label for this target kind (statement reads are paid per page). Logged `detail: 'reclassified'` (unhinted) or `'corrected'`.
- **Pasted text is capped before we know it is a statement.** `sourceFromText` (`useSharedDocumentIngest.ts:552-740`) truncates to `MAX_SHARE_TEXT_CHARS` (10 000, `share/types.ts:54`) and refuses above `MAX_SHARE_TEXT_CEILING` (32 000). A hinted paste bypasses that policy: `inAppSource` returns the full text for `hint === 'transactions'` after only the `MIN_SHARE_TEXT_CHARS` check, and the statement reader owns its own bounding (chunks). An UNHINTED paste keeps the existing bands, but the `text` arm of `FirstReadSource` gains `full?: string` (the trimmed original, set only when truncated) so the reclassified branch reads the whole statement rather than the capped copy. Above the ceiling the existing "too long" refusal stands; `shareTarget.text.tooLong.message` gains one sentence pointing at the Transactions tile so the honest recovery is stated rather than guessed.
- **Per-page reads and the meter.** Each page or chunk is one enclave call and one bean. SCB costs 5, BofA 1, Chase 1. The consent sheet states the count. The Lambda's `MAX_IMAGES = 8` (legacy arm only, `index.mjs:60`) is never approached (one image per call); the proxy's `FAMILY_LIMIT = 80/h` and `IP_LIMIT = 120/h` (`rateLimit.mjs:65-67`) leave ample room for `STATEMENT_MAX_PAGES = 12`. `MAX_EXTRACT_PAGES = 5` (`pdfExtractionImages.ts:26`) stays for other kinds; the statement reader never calls `pdfToExtractionImages` (see §3) so the cap is not bypassed, it is simply not on this path. Pages beyond `STATEMENT_MAX_PAGES` are reported as not read. The client text budget (`SHARE_TEXT_BUDGET`, 20/h managed) is owned by the spine (`textBudgetKey()` is a private helper at `:524-527` and must stay one; the statement service never touches it). The branch consumes it AFTER consent and BEFORE the first model call, one `consumeAttempt` per text chunk, using the unit count `prepareStatementUnits` returns: a refusal at chunk k ends the run with the existing `refuseForQuota` toast before any model call is made and before any bean is spent, which is cheaper and more honest than refusing mid-run. Image and PDF units are not text-budgeted, exactly as today.
- **Page classification needs a text layer.** `pdf.js getTextContent` is not used anywhere today; the classifier adds it. A scanned PDF without a text layer classifies nothing and every page is sent (up to the cap), which the sheet reports as "N pages, N reads". A photo or screenshot is always one read. The PDF is loaded ONCE: the classifier and the renderer share one `getDocument` (see §3), so the file is not parsed twice. Every page up to the cap is RENDERED in that single pass (rendering is on-device and costs no bean); only kept pages are SENT. A dropped page's rendered image travels with the read result as a prepared source, which is what makes "read it anyway" a one-call service action from the review rather than a second trip through the ingest spine (see §5).
- **`MODEL_LIST_MAX = 100`** (`extractionPrompt.ts:213`) bounds lines per call. The densest sample page has ~45 lines, so one page per call fits. Text chunks are bounded on BOTH axes: at most `MAX_SHARE_TEXT_CHARS` (10 000) characters AND at most `STATEMENT_CHUNK_MAX_LINES = 60` non-header lines per chunk, cut at line boundaries using `boundText` (never a bare `slice`, which can split a surrogate pair), with the CSV header row prepended to every chunk. The line bound matters because a CSV export has short lines (a 10 000-character chunk of Chase CSV is ~160 rows), so a character-only bound would routinely exceed `MODEL_LIST_MAX` and lose rows to the parser cap; 60 leaves headroom for multi-line descriptions. If a single call still returns 100 lines the chunk is flagged `capped` and reported.
- **Sign conventions differ per bank.** The model is told the account kind it detected (`bank | card`) and asked for `direction` explicitly (`out` = money leaving the family, `in` = money arriving) and `kind`. The client never infers direction from sign. A card `payment` is money in on the card account and is offered as a transfer.
- **Identical same-day lines are distinct** (Chase 3 × -5,000.00). The fingerprint carries a same-day ordinal; in-batch dedupe is by fingerprint only, never by amount+date+description.
- **A bank reference is not unique on its own.** BofA prints a 4-digit reference per line, which recurs across months; a fingerprint keyed on `account + reference` alone would mark a NEW line as already imported on a later statement and silently untick it. The fingerprint is therefore ONE formula for every line (§4): a hash over account, date, amount, direction, ordinal and a text part, where the text part is the bank reference when present (stable across re-reads) and the normalised description otherwise. There is no separate `ref:` path.
- **Amounts in a currency different from the account.** The account's currency wins for the stored `amount` (the statement's own column is already in the account currency on all three samples); the original foreign amount is display-only in the review and is not persisted in v1 (no field exists; noted as a follow-up).
- **`TransactionModal` resets `isReconciled` to `false` on every edit** (`TransactionModal.vue:566, 653`). This becomes a real bug once rows are reconciled. Fixed in this change: both sites write `props.transaction?.isReconciled ?? false` (new rows stay `false`).
- **Recurring propagation.** `syncLinkedTransactions` (`TransactionsPage.vue:599-640`) already skips reconciled rows, so a merged instance is protected from template edits. That is the intended behaviour and is documented in the help article.
- **`deduplicateRecurringTransactions`** (`recurringProcessor.ts:408-440`) deletes extras per `recurringItemId|date` without reversing balances. A merged-into-projection row uses the statement date, which may differ from the due date; the guard in `processRecurringItems` (`:55-60`) checks `recurringItemId` + due date, so a later run could materialise the due-date instance again beside the merged one. Two things close this hole together. First, the matcher offers a PROJECTION as a candidate only when the statement line's date falls in the same calendar month as the projection's due date (a real, already-materialised row has no such restriction), so every merged instance sits in its due month. Second, the processor's guard is widened NARROWLY: skip when a transaction for this `recurringItemId` exists on the due date (today's rule) OR when a **reconciled** transaction for it exists in the due date's month. Scoping the widening to reconciled rows keeps today's behaviour for every family that has never imported (a hand-moved unreconciled instance still materialises as before) while closing the exact hole a merge opens, and the month rule is the same one the projection suppression `TransactionsPage.vue:231-242` already applies, so the ledger view, the processor and the matcher agree on one definition of "this month's instance is accounted for". The known limit: a charge due on the 31st and posted on the 2nd will not match the projection; it is offered as a plain add, exactly as a hand-typed line is today. Stated in the help article.
- **No per-row toasts.** `wrapAsync` toasts every store throw and returns `undefined` rather than rethrowing (`useStoreActions.ts:53-101`). The commit does not loop `createTransaction`; adds go through one repository batch. Merges do loop `transactionsStore.updateTransaction`, whose own `wrapAsync` already toasts a failure (with the stack shipped to Slack) and returns `null` (`transactionsStore.ts:569`); the store counts the nulls, reports `merge-failed` ONCE with the count, and adds no toast of its own per row. `showToast` dedupes identical toasts, so a run of identical failures is one toast.
- **Commit order is fixed: the atomic add batch FIRST, a store reload SECOND, the per-row merges THIRD.** This is what keeps the result state machine small. The batch is all-or-nothing, so if it throws nothing has been written and the result is `failed` (retry from the same list). Only once the batch is in are `transactionsStore.loadTransactions()` and `accountsStore.loadAccounts()` called (the batch bypasses both stores' arrays), and only then do merges run: `createTransaction`'s first-row check (`:481`, `transactions.value.length === 0`) and `updateTransaction`'s `original` lookup (`:515`) both read the store array, so reloading BEFORE the merges is what stops a projection merge from seeing an empty store, firing a second `celebrate('first-transaction')`, or computing a balance cascade from stale state. Merge failures can then only ever produce `partial`, never a "some adds written, some not" state. If the batch read-back reports `unverified`, the commit STOPS before reloading or merging (rows may exist unseen; running merges on top would make the "do not re-import" advice half-true) and the user is told; a later re-import shows those adds as already imported by fingerprint and offers the merges again, so nothing is lost, only deferred.
- **Transfers in the add batch.** A transfer's destination credit needs `toAmount` (cross-currency), and the only authority for it is `transactionsStore.resolveTransferToAmount` (`transactionsStore.ts:243-262`, currently private, throws on a missing rate). It is exposed from the store and called for every transfer draft BEFORE the batch is built, so a missing rate fails the commit with nothing written and the store's existing `reportError` ('transactions.transfer-missing-rate') fires. Increments are computed with `signedAccountDelta` for the source and, for a transfer, the destination with `toAmount` (the same two calls `applyTransactionToBalances` makes, `:276-301`). **A transfer draft always has a destination.** A line is a transfer only once `toAccountId` is known (a detected counterpart, or an account the user picked); a card payment with no counterpart is an `income` draft carrying a transfer OFFER chip ("from ▾"), and choosing an account in that chip is what converts the draft. No invalid transfer can reach the commit, so there is nothing to validate there.
- **One decision field per line, not three.** The review state for a line is a single `decision: 'add' | 'merge' | 'skip'` beside an immutable `defaultDecision` the planner set. "Ticked" is derived (`decision !== 'skip'`), "keep both" on a familiar card IS `add` (the card labels it "keep both" because it has a `match`), and "overrides" is `decision !== defaultDecision`. An earlier draft carried `outcome`, `choice` and `ticked` as three mutable fields whose combinations had to be kept consistent by every action and every bulk action; collapsing them means the bulk bar, the tick button, the pair card, the totals, the commit and the telemetry all read one field and cannot disagree.
- **Changing the account resets decisions.** Fingerprints are keyed on the import's account (the header pick), so `setAccount` must re-plan, and a re-plan restores planner defaults. This is stated on the account row ("changing the account re-checks every line") rather than attempting to carry per-line edits across a re-plan, which would need a reconciliation of its own. A per-row account override in the inline editor changes only where that row is WRITTEN; it does not change the row's fingerprint (the fingerprint identifies the statement line, and the statement belongs to the header account), so no per-row re-hash is needed.
- **One assembly of `ExtractOptions`.** The tier, BYOK config, billable family id and `todayIso` are assembled inline today in `read()` (`useSharedDocumentIngest.ts:931-945`) and again in `useRecipeCapture.ts:445-447`. The statement branch and the store's "read it anyway" would be a third and fourth copy, so the assembly moves into `useAiCapability()` as `extractOptions({ grant, familyId, signal? })` (it already owns `tier` and `byokConfig`), and the two existing sites switch to it in the same change. The statement service receives the finished options and never calls a composable.
- **Do not build:** balance reconciliation to zero (follow-up row; the extractor still returns opening and closing balances so that follow-up has data), bank connections, scheduled imports, reconciling outside an import, renaming `canViewFinances`, a persisted import history surface, batch undo.

## Approach

The design follows the calendar import (`calendarImportStore.ts` + `planImport.ts`): a pure planner that writes nothing, a small store that owns phases, one atomic repository commit with read-back verification, and a drawer that renders from the plan. It extends the magic beans pipeline by the documented checklist (`magicDestinations.ts:10-24`) plus the registry-enumerating sites the checklist does not list. Every helper named below was checked against the codebase; where a near-copy already exists it is extracted and shared rather than re-implemented.

Layering rule for this feature, stated once so every section below obeys it: pure modules in `src/utils/statement/` know nothing about Vue, stores or the AI service; `src/services/ai/statementExtraction.ts` knows the AI service and pdf.js but never the ingest spine's state, the text budget, or any store; the spine's statement branch and `statementImportStore` are the only two orchestrators, and the components render from the store. No module reaches "upward" into a layer above it.

### 1. Data model (`src/types/models.ts`)

Add to `Transaction` (all optional, additive):

```ts
statementDescription?: string; // the bank's raw line text, shown as "on your statement as"
importFingerprint?: string;    // see §4; a later import matching it shows "already added"
importId?: UUID;               // groups the rows of one import run
importSource?: 'pdf' | 'image' | 'csv' | 'text';
importPeriod?: { from: ISODateString; to: ISODateString }; // statement period, YYYY-MM-DD
```

Add to `Settings`: `aiStatementConsentAcknowledgedAt?: ISODateString` (beside `skipDocumentConsentPrompt`, `models.ts:1996`).

`TransactionModal` (edit path, `:566` and `:653`) preserves `props.transaction?.isReconciled ?? false` instead of writing `false`; `TransactionViewEditModal` shows `statementDescription` under a `transactions.onStatementAs` label when present, next to the existing reconciled badge (`:619-626`).

### 2. Registry: the new kind `transactions` and reader `statement`

### 4. Planner, matcher, fingerprint (pure, `src/utils/statement/`)

`fingerprint.ts`: `statementFingerprint(accountId, line, ordinal): Promise<string>` = `sha256HexOfParts([accountId, date, amount.toFixed(2), direction, line.reference?.trim() || normaliseMerchant(description), String(ordinal)], '\0')` (`encoding.ts:174`, which throws when `crypto.subtle` is unavailable; the store's `start()` catch classifies that as `plan_failed`). ONE formula for every line: the bank reference, when present, stands in for the description because it is stable across re-reads where OCR of a description may not be, but it never stands in for the date or amount (a 4-digit BofA reference recurs across statements; see Notes). `accountId` is the import's header account, never a per-row override (see Notes). `ordinal` is the line's index among lines in the same unit sharing date + amount + direction + text part (0-based), so identical same-day lines stay distinct and stable across re-reads of the same statement.

`transfers.ts`: `suggestTransfer(line, otherAccountsTx)`: for `kind ∈ {payment, transfer}` find an opposite-direction row in another family account with equal amount (2 dp) within 3 days; return `{ otherAccountId, transactionId }` and the planner writes the draft as `type: 'transfer'` with that `toAccountId`. `planStatementImport.ts` (named to differ from `utils/calendar/planImport.ts`, so a search or an import path never has two `planImport.ts` files to tell apart): `planStatementImport({ result, accountId, accounts, transactions, recurringItems, fingerprints, importMeta }): StatementImportPlan` producing `candidates: StatementCandidate[]` (one per line) and `notices` (`droppedPages`, `pagesBeyondCap`, `unitsFailed`, `capped`, `overlapAlready`). ```ts

  fingerprint: string;
  draft: CreateTransactionInput; // the ONLY copy of what would be written
  match?: FamiliarMatch; // present ⇒ rendered as a pair card

## Observability Coverage

Surface `statement-import` for the store/planner/commit; the existing `magic-beans-capture` / `share-target-ingest` events carry `kind: 'transactions'` through the shared pipeline. Per run, counts only, existing allowlisted keys (`action`, `kind`, `count`, `detail`, `error_code`); no new context keys, so no store-declaration change.

- **Events** (`logEvent`, `message === context.action`):
  - `pages_classified` info, `count` = kept, `detail` = `dropped:<n>|beyond_cap:<n>|no_text_layer|classify_failed:<n>`
  - `read_unit_failed` warn, `error_code`, `detail` = `unit:<n>` (`count` is reserved for counts, never an index)
  - `read_complete` info, `count` = lines, `detail` = `units:<n>|failed:<n>|capped:<n>|source:<pdf|image|csv|text|prepared>`
  - `read_dropped_page` info (a "read it anyway"), `count` = lines gained, or `read_unit_failed` with `detail: 'dropped_page'` on failure
  - `reclassified` info (unhinted read, or a correction, turned out to be a statement; `detail` = `unhinted|corrected`)
  - `consent_deferred_wrong_kind` info via `notReady` (should-not-happen; a deferred grant reached a non-statement path)
  - `merchant_memory` info, `count` = merchants sent
  - `plan_built` info, ONE event per plan, `count` = lines, `detail` = `familiar:<n>|already:<n>|transfer:<n>|bad_category:<n>|no_account` (packed into `detail` the same way `pages_classified` and `read_complete` already pack theirs, instead of four sibling events that each carried one number)
  - `plan_failed` error via `reportError` (`error_code`)
  - `commit_mix` info, `detail` = `add:<n>|merge:<n>|keep:<n>|skip:<n>|overrides:<n>` (`keep` = `decision === 'add'` on a matched candidate; overrides = `decision !== defaultDecision`: the matcher-quality signal)
  - `commit` info, `count` = added + merged
  - `commit_failed`: `reportError` severity `critical` for `error_code: 'verify-missing'` (rows may exist unseen; user told not to re-import; merges not attempted) and severity `error` for `batch-write-threw`, `transfer-rate-missing` (nothing written) and `merge-failed` (count of failed merges in `count`, flow closes with a counted toast); `balance-increment-skipped` warning with `count`
  - `perfTiming.record('statement-import.read', ms, { count: units })`, `('statement-import.plan', ms, { count: lines })`
- **Failure modes covered**: classifier throws or no text layer (`pages_classified` detail); a page the model cannot read (`read_unit_failed`); every unit failing (the shared extraction toast + `read_complete` with `count: 0`); quota/consent/rate-limit refusals (existing `refused`/`consent_declined`/`failed` with `error_code`; a text-budget refusal fires before any model call, so `read_complete` is absent and `refused` is the last event of that run); planner or hashing throw (`plan_failed`); no account matched (`plan_built` detail `no_account`, save disabled until chosen); the wrong account chosen (visible as a high `overrides` share); batch write failure vs invisible rows (`commit_failed` codes); missing FX rate on a transfer add (`transfer-rate-missing`, plus the store's existing report); merge side-effect failures (`merge-failed` with count, each already toasted with its stack by `wrapAsync`); an account deleted mid-commit (`balance-increment-skipped`); a "read it anyway" that fails (`read_unit_failed` `dropped_page`). No bare `catch`; every `catch` classifies, logs, and either falls back (classifier page, single unit) or surfaces (everything else) with developer guidance on the console where the fix is not obvious from the toast (PDF load, unknown category id).
- **Success-path signal**: `read_complete`, `plan_built`, `commit` fire every run, so read/plan/commit rates and the familiar/override ratios are measurable for alerting.

**Deviations from the plan, each deliberate:**

1. **Matcher: a category-agreement signal.** The plan's own acceptance example could not pass its
   weight table (Mortgage 2,000 on the 1st vs HOME LOAN 2,305.17 on the 14th scored 0.40 against a
   0.6 threshold). `MATCH_WEIGHTS.category = 0.25` applies when the line's resolved category (a
   valid model hint or merchant memory, never the `other_*` fallback) equals the candidate's; the
   example scores 0.65. A candidate with no amount signal scores 0. Consequence: the acceptance
   example needs the model (or memory) to file the line under the same category as the family's
   row. Tune on real statements via the one constant block.
2. **Category ids travel in `ExtractionContext.categories`**, not as a hardcoded list in the three
   prompt copies (the way the activity taxonomy is). No sync test against `categories.ts` is needed
   in the Lambda/spike copies. ADR-030's update says app taxonomy rides with the merchant memory.
3. **Counterpart transfers merge into the existing row.** The plan had the draft become a transfer
   ADD, which would have left the family's typed "Credit card bill" expense beside it and
   double-counted the money. Now the counterpart is the candidate's `match` (a pair card, "Records
   it as one transfer between the two accounts") and merging converts that row into the one
   transfer; the draft stays plain income/expense, so "keep both" never makes a second transfer.
4. **`editDraft` converts or refuses cross-currency source changes** (found by the test pass): a
   "Paid from?" pick or an account change to an account in another currency converts the amount
   with the family's rates, or is refused with a toast when there is no rate.
5. **Text statements are capped at `STATEMENT_MAX_UNITS` (12) chunks**, the same cap as pages, with
   the remainder reported as not read. The plan capped only PDF pages.
6. **The text budget is consumed all-or-nothing** (`consumeAttempts`), so a statement needing more
   chunks than the budget has left is refused before any read rather than part-way through.
7. **A PDF whose classifier keeps no page reads every page** (logged `none_kept`) rather than
   reading nothing.
8. **`useAccountOptionGroups`** was extracted from `TransactionModal` (the review needs the same
   grouped, balance-labelled account picker). **`TickButton`'s prop is `label`**, not `ariaLabel`
   (vue-tsc + `vue/attribute-hyphenation`).
9. **No terraform apply.** The Lambda files changed (prompt mirror, `SHARE_KINDS`, the legacy
   builder call) matter only to legacy-arm clients, which never send this kind; the sealed arm the
   app uses builds its prompt client-side and `TASK_RE` already accepted `statement`. Ships with the
   next infra apply.

**Review: two rounds at `high` (the ceiling without greg).**

- **Round 1 (10 findings, 9 fixed):** projection merges dropped `loanId`/`activityId`; the plan's
  month-wide processor guard suppressed every other weekly/daily instance in a month; a
  deliberately ticked already-added line was silently not saved; a re-plan kept `merge` with no
  match; a multi-screenshot share read only the first image; unsorted candidates drew one day
  twice; "read it anyway" raced confirm/close and wiped the review on failure; merchant memory
  could carry family members' names (now screened) and is framed and quoted as data in the
  prompt; the transfer chip always said "from", and every refused edit claimed a missing rate.
  **Not fixed, by design:** rows that already carry an `importFingerprint` stay out of fuzzy
  matching. An imported row is already reconciled to one bank line, so merging a second line into
  it would silently rewrite a real transaction; a drifted duplicate (pending → posted with a
  different date or description) instead shows as a visible plain add in the review.
- **Round 2 (10 findings, 10 fixed), and a structural change:** five findings were in the same
  place, "which due date does this recurring row stand for", which three sites each decided for
  themselves and got differently. That decision now lives in ONE helper,
  `src/utils/recurringInstance.ts` (`recurringDueDate ?? date`), used by the processor's
  materialisation guard, both duplicate sweeps (`transactionsStore.deduplicateRecurring`,
  `recurringProcessor.deduplicateRecurringTransactions`), the ledger's dedupe on
  `TransactionsPage` and the import's projection filter. A merged row records `recurringDueDate`
  whether it was a projection or an already-materialised row. The month-wide rule and the
  matcher's same-month projection restriction are gone. Also fixed: a kept `merge` is honoured
  only while it points at the SAME row; a deliberate tick on an already-added line survives a
  re-plan; the read-it-anyway restore never clobbers a concurrent account change; a corrected PDF
  re-reads the original (photos keep the prepared images, which carry every shared image);
  member-name screening runs before the 150 cap and covers two-letter names; one
  `lineDirection` helper for the row, the card and the editor.
- **Known limit, recorded:** a long PASTED text corrected to Transactions after an unhinted read
  is still its first 10 000 characters (the envelope keeps no fuller copy). Choosing Transactions
  before reading avoids it, and the too-long toast now says so.
- **Not reviewed a third time.** Round 2's fixes include the structural move above and touch
  money paths (processor, sweeps); a third `/code-review` scoped to them is greg's call.
