## User Story

As a family who has been using beanies free during beta, I want the app to tell me plainly what my plan is, when my trial ends and what changes when it does, so that the day it changes is one I saw coming and chose.

## Context

The marketing site publicly commits to a commercial model the app cannot honour. `/pricing` promises a 90-day full trial, read-only after it, magic-beans quotas (1/day trial, 1/month basic, 10/day full, unlimited on your own key), $30/yr basic and $84.99/yr (or $9.99/mo) with AI, half price for life for pre-v1 families and $1/mo for the first ten. The app has no plan state, no trial clock, no read-only mode, no usage meter and no way to take money; a family reaching day 91 notices nothing. Closing that gap gates v1 revenue.

Decisions taken 2026-09-30 (greg), after a pre-research session (`docs/prompts/2026-09/2026-09-30-pricing-95-pre-research.md`):

- **One payment gateway: Stripe**, from the signed-in web app only. No Apple IAP, no Google Play Billing, no RevenueCat, no in-app link, button, price or plan chooser on iOS/Android (Netflix / Hey pattern; Apple 3.1.3(f), Google Play "no encouraging language").
- **Checkout surface**: a beanies plan page (Settings → Plan) hosting **Stripe Embedded Checkout**; the hosted **Customer Portal** on a custom domain for cancel / card / invoices. Custom Elements ruled out.
- **Stripe never sees the trial**: the 90 days is our server clock; subscribing during the trial bills immediately.
- Read-only blocks **all** family-data writes; lapse = when Stripe marks the subscription unpaid or cancelled; a cached paid entitlement is honoured **14 days** offline; the first ten are marked by greg with an admin script.
- **Gate + dry-run until v1**: every pricing surface behind the Settings feature gate; every enforcement path runs dry-run in prod first (compute, log the would-be outcome, take no action), soak, then flip.
- Stripe account: NJL Solutions Pte. Ltd., sandbox `acct_1UKmhOLhz3C06djX` connected via the Stripe MCP; "Pick what you need" (not Managed Payments); Stripe Tax threshold monitoring only; Radar Lite.

## Requirements

1. **Entitlement is server-computed.** Every client (web, iOS, Android) learns its plan, trial and read-only state from the registry GET it already makes once per session (`syncStore.checkCanonicalPod`, `syncStore.ts:4959-4996`). The client never computes the trial; it only applies the offline rule to a cached answer.
2. **Trial clock.** `trialEndsAt = max(RegistryEntry.createdAt, V1_LAUNCH_AT) + 90 days`, computed on the server from the server-stamped `createdAt` and a single Terraform variable `V1_LAUNCH_AT`. Unset (today) means "beta: no trial end". Everyone, beta families included, gets a fresh 90 days from launch (decided).
3. **Read-only** blocks all family-data writes at `mutate()` (`docClient.ts:1279`), with an explicit `system` allowlist so sign-in stamps, sync bookkeeping, roster heal, notification acks, merges, compaction and migrations keep working; recurring processing is paused and catches up on the next writable run. Export, sign-in, sync and appearance settings always work.
4. **Lapse** = Stripe subscription status `unpaid` or `canceled` (after Smart Retries). `past_due` is still active.
5. **Offline**: a cached `active` entitlement is honoured for 14 days from `fetchedAt`, then treated as read-only until the next successful refresh. A cached `trial` expires on its `trialEndsAt` regardless of connectivity.
6. **Magic-beans allowance** enforced in the ai-extract Lambda before the read: trial 1/day, basic 1/month, full 10/day; BYOK and on-device never reach the Lambda so are exempt by construction; the free dedupe task (`f`) is never counted. A usage endpoint lets the client show what is left.
7. **One gateway, Stripe**, web only: a `billing` Lambda creates Embedded Checkout sessions (server picks the Price and any cohort discount), verifies Stripe webhooks, and hands out one per-family bearer secret on first payment (the "plan token") that gates the Customer Portal session and the paid AI allowance.
8. **Native**: no purchase path at all. The read-only band shows greg's final copy and Export only. The Plan page route is unreachable on iOS/Android.
9. **Grandfathering**: `cohort` (`pre_v1` | `first_ten` | null) is explicit data in the billing table, written by an admin script at launch (pre_v1 for every row existing at that moment) and by greg for the first ten. `pre_v1` → 50% forever coupon; `first_ten` → dedicated $1/mo and $12/yr Prices.
10. **Gate + dry-run until v1**: the `pricing` feature flag gates every UI surface; `BILLING_ENFORCE=false` and `AI_ALLOWANCE_ENFORCE=false` make both Lambdas compute and log the would-be decision without acting; the client honours `entitlement.enforced`. Soak, then flip in a documented runbook order.
11. **Prices are one source**: `PRICES`, `TRIAL_DAYS`, `MAGIC_BEANS` move from `web/src/lib/pricing.ts` to `packages/brand/pricing.ts` (`@beanies/brand/pricing`) so the app and the marketing site cannot drift. Stripe Prices are addressed by `lookup_key` (`<cohort>.<plan>.<interval>.<currency>`, set once per Price in the Dashboard, identical in sandbox and live); the client sends `{plan, interval, currency}` and never a price.
12. **Observability**: every entitlement decision, allowance decision, webhook, checkout and claim emits a structured event; in dry-run the would-be outcome is the event.

## Important Notes & Caveats

- **Billing state lives in its own table**, `beanies-billing-<env>` (hash key `familyId`), owned by the billing Lambda. The registry Lambda only reads it. This removes the registry PUT/DELETE row-rebuild trap (`registry/index.mjs:271-387`, `:466-482`) and any race between a client PUT and a webhook: nothing in the registry row needs preserving. A DELETE tombstone leaves the billing row alone.
- **No `stripe` npm dependency.** Every Lambda is a zero-dependency `archive_file` on `nodejs20.x` and nothing ships `node_modules`. The integration needs five REST calls (list Prices by lookup_key, create Checkout Session, retrieve Checkout Session, create Portal Session, retrieve Subscription) and one HMAC check, so the billing Lambda uses Node's `fetch` against `https://api.stripe.com/v1/...` (form-encoded) and `crypto.createHmac` + `timingSafeEqual` for `Stripe-Signature` (scheme `v1`, `t.payload`, 300 s tolerance). Introducing a bundling step for one dependency is the larger change.
- **No cross-Lambda shared directory.** `computeEntitlement` is needed by exactly one Lambda, the registry GET: the billing claim does not return an entitlement (the client calls `entitlementStore.refresh()` after claim) and ai-extract only needs the plan tier, not the trial clock. So `entitlement.mjs` is a sibling file in `infrastructure/lambda/registry/`, only the registry `archive_file` changes, and ai-extract's packaging (`modules/ai-extract/main.tf:163-165`, `source_dir`) is untouched. A `lambda/shared/` directory would be the first in the repo (six self-contained Lambdas today) and would force every zip using it onto a `dynamic "source"`/`fileset` shape for one file.
- **Raw body for the webhook.** API Gateway v2 may deliver the body base64-encoded (`event.isBase64Encoded`); no handler today checks it. The webhook handler must decode before verifying and must verify the exact bytes.
- **The plan token is a bearer secret, not identity, and it is NOT the family key** (`familyKey` is the pod's encryption key in ~240 places under `src/`, e.g. `models.ts:176,189,223`; a second "family key" would be indistinguishable in grep, logs and review). It is generated on first successful claim, stored only as a sha256 in the billing row, returned once, and kept in the family's encrypted Automerge doc (`settings.planToken`) so every member's device has it. It gates two things only: Portal sessions (so a forged `familyId` cannot cancel a stranger's plan) and the `full` AI allowance (so a forged `familyId` cannot spend a paying family's beans). Trial and basic allowances stay keyed on `familyId` alone; that exposure is one read a day or a month and is accepted as the honest gate.
- **The Automerge doc is user-editable**, so the token can be wiped by a member, and, more likely, overwritten by a concurrent `setSettings` from another device: the named op replaces the whole singleton (`docOps.ts:561-565`), `saveSettings` merges from a read then replaces (`settingsRepository.ts:64-80`), and `syncStore` writes device sync bookkeeping through it on every load (`syncStore.ts:1157-1162`). Two devices replacing `settings` between syncs converge on one object and the other's fields are gone, a just-claimed `planToken` included. Accepted for v1 (a dedicated doc field is a schema migration) but made **visible**: `entitlementStore` logs `plan_token_missing` (warning, once per session) whenever `entitlement.plan` is set and `settings.planToken` is absent, with the `--reissue-token` remediation in the console line, so greg re-issues before the family notices a `basic` allowance. Portal and `full` allowance simply behave as basic until then. The recovery path is manual: `scripts/billing-cohort.mjs --reissue-token <familyId>`. No self-service re-claim in v1.
- **`lastLoginAt` writes share the `familyMembers` collection with real member edits**, so the gate cannot allowlist by collection. The allowlist is an explicit `opts.system: true` set by the system callers listed in the Approach, plus the named op `setSettings` (every settings write, `settingsRepository.ts:81`, which is also what keeps theme/language/text size and the device sync bookkeeping working; `syncStore.ts:1157-1162` already says that bookkeeping does not belong in the shared doc), plus the collections `notificationReads` and `overlapAcknowledgments`.
- **Developer direction is mandatory on every failure path** (house style: `countUsage.mjs` `COUNT_FAILED_REMEDIATION`, `settingsStore.ts:440-444`). Every new Lambda error prefix carries a fixed remediation sentence naming the env var, table or IAM action to check; every client failure log names the fix or the script to run.
- **`mergeRemoteEnvelope`, `applyChanges`, `loadSnapshot`, `compactDoc`, `migrateDoc` bypass `mutate()`** and must keep doing so: they are how a read-only family still receives a peer's edits. No gate there.
- **The AI day key is UTC** (`countUsage.mjs:42-44`). Decision recorded below (Assumption 9): keep UTC and show the reset time in local time in the UI. Changing the key is a data migration for no product gain.
- **The pre-check races the count**: allowance is checked (GetItem) before the read and counted after (existing `closeRead`); two simultaneous reads can overrun by one. Accepted; the hourly rate limiter bounds it.
- **Stripe handles receipts and dunning emails** (Dashboard: "Successful payments" and "Failed payments" customer emails on). Our only email is the trial-ending reminder, which needs the SES sender that #115 has pre-planned but not built. It is Phase 6 and can ship after v1 without blocking enforcement.
- **Do not overload `aiTier`** (privacy tier). Plan is a separate concept.
- **App Review wording is fixed** (greg's copy). Never add a link, URL, price or purchase verb to native surfaces. Store metadata: no In-App Purchases.
- **`docs/plans/2026-09-10-pricing-page.md` is stale** on "free tier after trial"; do not read it as truth.
- **The client can never be forced to enforce** (update floor is prompt-only). Server-side AI denial is the only enforcement a stale client obeys; that is fine for an honesty gate.

## Approach

Mockup: `docs/mockups/pricing-plan-and-read-only-2026-09-30.html` (read in full; every token below comes from the theme skill, not the mockup's CSS).

### Phase 1: entitlement computed on the server (dry-run from day one)

**Entitlement module** `infrastructure/lambda/registry/entitlement.mjs` (pure, no AWS imports; the only place the trial/lapse rules live; imported by `index.mjs` and by `lambdaContractParity.test.ts`):

```js
export function computeEntitlement({ createdAt, billing, launchAt, now, enforce }) → {
  state: 'beta' | 'trial' | 'active' | 'read_only',
  reason: 'no_launch' | 'in_trial' | 'subscribed' | 'trial_ended' | 'lapsed',
  plan: 'basic' | 'full' | null, cohort, trialEndsAt, currentPeriodEnd, enforced: enforce, serverTime: now }
```

Rules: no `launchAt` → `beta`. `billing.status in ('active','trialing','past_due')` → `active` with `plan`. Else, with `trialEndsAt = billing.trialEndsAt ?? max(createdAt, launchAt) + 90d` (the optional per-family override is how the gate gets soaked in prod before launch + 90 days, and the only support lever for extending a trial), `now < trialEndsAt` → `trial`. Else `read_only` with `trial_ended` or `lapsed` (if a subscription ever existed). `enforced` is passed through so the client can display state while dry-run.

**Registry Lambda** (`infrastructure/lambda/registry/index.mjs`): the GET (`:75-93`) additionally `GetItem`s `beanies-billing-<env>` and returns `{...row, entitlement}` from `computeEntitlement`. The billing `GetItem` is wrapped in its own try/catch inside the GET arm: the handler's outer try (`:72`) would otherwise turn a billing-table blip into a 500 on the pointer lookup that recovery-from-registry depends on. Failure → `entitlement: null` + `console.error('[registry] entitlement_unavailable — check BILLING_TABLE_NAME and dynamodb:GetItem on the billing table', err)`. New env: `BILLING_TABLE_NAME`, `V1_LAUNCH_AT` (optional), `BILLING_ENFORCE` (`'true'|'false'`). Logs one line per GET: `entitlement_computed {state, reason, enforced}` (the soak signal). IAM: `dynamodb:GetItem` on the billing table. PUT and DELETE are untouched. The billing `GetItem` always targets the single prod billing table regardless of the request Origin; the registry's own dev/prod split (`tableForOrigin`, `registry/index.mjs:26`) is not extended to billing. A localhost family therefore reads a billing row that does not exist and computes `trial`/`beta`, which is the right answer: the billing Lambda is prod-only (webhooks carry no Origin) and checkout already refuses `DEV_ORIGINS`.

**Terraform** (`infrastructure/modules/billing/main.tf`, new): the billing DynamoDB table (`familyId` hash key, PITR + deletion protection in prod like the usage table, `ai-extract/main.tf:65-135` pattern), the billing Lambda + log group, routes on the shared HTTP API (`POST /billing/checkout-session`, `POST /billing/claim`, `POST /billing/portal-session`, `POST /billing/webhook`, each with its own `route_settings` throttle), and variables `stripe_secret_key`, `stripe_webhook_secret`, `stripe_pre_v1_coupon`, `v1_launch_at`, `billing_enforce`, `ai_allowance_enforce`. The billing Lambda also receives `REGISTRY_TABLE_NAME` (the prod registry table) and `dynamodb:GetItem` on it (checkout reads `ownerEmail` and the tombstone state); it never writes the registry. Root `main.tf` wires the module and passes `billing_table_name` into registry and ai-extract. `infrastructure/.beanies-tf.env.example` gains the `TF_VAR_*` lines. Only the registry `archive_file` changes (Assumption 7).

**Billing table row**: `{ familyId, stripeCustomerId, stripeSubscriptionId, status, plan, interval, currency, currentPeriodEnd, cohort, trialEndsAt?, planTokenHash, tokenClaimedAt, updatedAt }` (`plan`/`interval`/`currency` derived from the Price `lookup_key`). **Write discipline**: the row has three writers with disjoint fields (webhook: subscription fields; claim: token hash, claim time, ids; script: `cohort`, `trialEndsAt`, `planTokenHash`). Every writer uses `UpdateItem` with `SET` on its own attributes only, never `PutItem`: a `PutItem` from the webhook would erase `cohort` and the token hash, one from the script would erase the subscription. The unit test for each writer asserts the other writers' attributes survive.

**Admin script** `scripts/billing-cohort.mjs`, following `scripts/migrate-registry-dev-rows.mjs` (AWS SDK from devDependencies, greg's credentials, **dry-run by default and `--apply` to write**, prints every row it would touch): `--snapshot-pre-v1` scans the registry and writes `cohort=pre_v1` into billing rows for every non-tombstoned family (idempotent, never downgrades `first_ten`); `--first-ten <familyId>` sets `first_ten`; `--trial-ends-at <familyId> <iso>` sets the override (greg's own test family on day one, and support); `--reissue-token <familyId>` prints a fresh plan token and stores its hash (the manual recovery path).

### Phase 3: the write gate and the read-only band

The gate passes when `opts.system`, or the op is `named:setSettings`, or its collection (recursively for `batch`) is in `SYSTEM_COLLECTIONS = ['notificationReads', 'overlapAcknowledgments']`. Importing the store into `docClient` is a cycle risk; `docClient` exposes `setWriteGate(fn)` and `entitlementStore` installs it on init (the `setLocalChangeHandler` inversion, `:286`).

### Phase 5: Stripe (billing Lambda, Plan page, Embedded Checkout, Portal)

**Billing Lambda** `infrastructure/lambda/billing/index.mjs` (+ `stripeApi.mjs` thin REST client, `webhookSignature.mjs`, `__tests__/*.test.mjs` on `node:test` with mocked `fetch`):

- Routing and auth: route on `event.requestContext.http.path`. The three client routes require `x-api-key` first thing, exactly as the sibling Lambdas do (`registry/index.mjs:61-64`, `ai-extract/index.mjs:125`); `/billing/webhook` is exempt, its authentication is the signature, and a shared bundle key on that route would add nothing. This is the first Lambda in the repo with a route that skips the key, so the exemption carries a comment saying why.
- `stripeApi.mjs` exports one `STRIPE_API_VERSION` constant (choose a version ≥ `2025-06-30.basil`, the first with flexible billing mode; from `2025-09-30.clover` flexible is the default and `billing_mode[type]=flexible` becomes redundant but harmless) and sends it as `Stripe-Version` on every call; the Dashboard webhook endpoint is created on the same version. One `safeEqual(a, b)` helper (length check, then `crypto.timingSafeEqual` on byte buffers; `timingSafeEqual` throws on unequal lengths) is used by the signature check (accept if any `v1=` entry matches; the header may carry several), the claim's `client_secret` comparison, and the portal and ai-extract hash comparisons: one comparison to review, none by `===`.
- `POST /billing/checkout-session` `{ familyId, plan, interval, currency }` (x-api-key as today): refuses with 403 `code: 'dev_origin'` when the request `Origin` is in `DEV_ORIGINS` (the module holds one Stripe key pair; a localhost checkout against live keys would take real money; the registry's `tableForOrigin`, `index.mjs:26-28`, is the precedent for reading origin); reads registry (must exist, not tombstoned; `ownerEmail` for `customer_email`) and billing (`cohort`, existing `stripeCustomerId`); refuses if a subscription is already `active`; resolves the Price by `lookup_key` `${cohort ?? 'list'}.${plan}.${interval}.${currency}` via `GET /v1/prices?lookup_keys[]=…&active=true` (memoised per Lambda instance; a miss is `checkout_refused {reason: 'price_missing'}` with the remediation "create a Price with this lookup_key in the Stripe Dashboard"), so no Price id lives in Terraform, there is no nested JSON variable and no reverse map: the webhook reads `price.lookup_key` straight off the subscription item; for `pre_v1` adds `discounts[0][coupon]=<STRIPE_PRE_V1_COUPON>`; creates the session: `mode=subscription`, `ui_mode=embedded`, `redirect_on_completion=never` (no `return_url`; the page stays put), `client_reference_id=familyId`, `metadata[familyId]`, `subscription_data[metadata][familyId]`, `customer` or `customer_email`, `billing_mode[type]=flexible`; returns `{ sessionId, clientSecret }`.
- `POST /billing/claim` `{ familyId, sessionId, clientSecret }`: retrieves the session by id (no parsing of the secret's undocumented `cs_…_secret_…` shape), requires `session.client_secret === clientSecret`, `client_reference_id === familyId` and `status === 'complete'`; upserts customer/subscription ids; if `tokenClaimedAt` is null generates the plan token (32 random bytes, base64url), stores its hash, sets `tokenClaimedAt`, returns `{ planToken }`; a second claim returns `{}` (200). The client's `entitlementStore.refresh()` is the one source of entitlement; claim does not compute one. Deliberately independent of webhook ordering. A tab closed mid-payment still lands the subscription via the webhook; the token is then the manual reissue path (`--reissue-token`).
- `POST /billing/portal-session` `{ familyId, planToken }`: `sha256(planToken)` must equal the row's hash (the token travels in the body, not a custom header: the shared HTTP API's preflight allows only `Content-Type` and `x-api-key`, `registry/main.tf:170`, and every Lambda echoes the same two, so `x-plan-token` would fail CORS; the body is also how ai-extract already receives it, one convention); creates a Customer Portal session with `return_url` to the Plan page; returns `{ url }`.
- `POST /billing/webhook`: raw-body signature check (decode `isBase64Encoded` first). Subscribes to **three** event types only: `customer.subscription.created|updated|deleted`. One handler covers all three: resolve `familyId` from `data.object.metadata.familyId`, then **ignore the event's snapshot** and `GET /v1/subscriptions/{data.object.id}` (the retrieve call in `stripeApi.mjs`) so the row is always written from Stripe's current state, which is what makes delivery order irrelevant (Stripe's own guidance: snapshot events record `created` in seconds, distinct events can share a timestamp, do not use `created` for ordering; a same-second `subscription.created` with status `incomplete` could otherwise overwrite an `updated` `active` that arrived first). Write `status`, `plan` + `interval` + `currency` (from `items.data[0].price.lookup_key`), `currentPeriodEnd` (`items.data[0].current_period_end`; the subscription-level field was removed in `2025-03-31.basil`), `stripeCustomerId`, `stripeSubscriptionId`, `updatedAt` via `UpdateItem SET`. Duplicate and out-of-order deliveries converge on the retrieved state: no `evt#` idempotency ledger, no second row shape in the billing table (the trap `ddb.mjs:101` documents for the usage table, and it would have made the cohort script's scan filter `evt#` rows forever). `invoice.paid` / `invoice.payment_failed` / `checkout.session.completed` are not subscribed: a failed payment surfaces as `past_due` → `unpaid` via `subscription.updated`, a paid invoice as `active`, and checkout completion is the claim step; a tab closed mid-payment is still landed by `subscription.created`. Responses: 200 once the row is written or the event is deliberately ignored (unknown type, no `metadata.familyId`); **500** when the retrieve or the DynamoDB write fails, so Stripe retries (up to three days in live mode) and `webhook_apply_failed` pages once rather than the row silently drifting; 400 on a signature failure (never retried, correct). In dry-run nothing changes: the billing row is data, enforcement is the flag.
- Errors: every `fetch` in `stripeApi.mjs` carries `AbortSignal.timeout(10_000)` (the precedent Lambdas call `fetch` with no timeout, `oauth/index.mjs:157,189`; do not copy that); non-2xx, abort and network throw all become `billing_upstream_error {action, http_status|0}` with remediation text ("check STRIPE_SECRET_KEY, the Stripe status page, and the Lambda's outbound network") and a 502 `code: 'billing_upstream'`; the client shows `plan.error.checkoutUnavailable` with "try again" and a support address.

**Local harness** `infrastructure/lambda/billing/local.mjs` (added 2026-10-01, greg's call: after the live-key flip, sandbox testing happens on the dev machine, never as a second key set in prod or GitHub).
